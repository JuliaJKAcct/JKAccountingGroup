# Costs, fees & scope

What things actually cost, how a job gets priced, and what is — and isn't — inside a fee.

## LN-21 — Florida late reemployment-tax reports: $25 per month, per report, plus interest and fees on the penalty itself
- **Tags:** florida · penalties · payroll
- **Certainty:** Established
- **Star:** no
- **Added:** 2026-08-06
- **Updated:** 2026-08-06
- **Came from:** Florida DOR on the Tsminibears account, 2026-08-06
- **Detail:** [`tsminibears.md` §5](../../client-intelligence/clients/tsminibears.md)

**What happened.** This had only been an unverified reading of the RT-6N instructions until the
agency stated it directly. Note the part that cuts against intuition: **interest and certain fees
accrue on the penalty amounts**, so "zero wages → no tax → no interest" is wrong. On Tsminibears
it came to ≈$842.50 for two quarters, from a company that never paid a dollar of wages.

**The rule.** The charge is per report and per month, so **the total grows until the day the
returns are actually submitted** — any figure quoted on a call is an estimate, and worse, that
one came from a representative with **no POA on file**, so it was never read off the account.
**Get the real number from the DOR website:** each delinquent return shows its own cost when you
go to submit it, which also gives the true list of outstanding periods.

## LN-22 — When a cleanup is quoted separately: cap the hours, take an advance, then track them
- **Tags:** pricing · scope · cleanup
- **Certainty:** Established
- **Star:** yes
- **Added:** 2026-08-06
- **Updated:** 2026-08-06
- **Came from:** Gossip Miami — building a 2025 P&L from bank statements
- **Detail:** [`gossip-miami.md` §4/§6](../../client-intelligence/clients/gossip-miami.md)

**What happened.** A tax-prep-only client with no books needed a P&L built from bank statements
and QuickBooks access. It was quoted separately from the return, **capped at 10 hours**, with an
advance paid up front — then the hours get tracked and the balance invoiced when it closes.

**The rule.** That's the shape for any open-ended cleanup: **separate quote · hour cap · advance ·
tracked hours.** But know what the cap does and doesn't do — it protects the **fee**, not the
**calendar**. On this job the binding constraint was never the hours; it was the documents the
client still owed (statements, check images, reconnecting a bank feed). When the client owns the
inputs, they own the schedule.

## LN-23 — Tax preparation does not include preparing the client's financial statements
- **Tags:** pricing · proposals · scope
- **Certainty:** Firm rule
- **Star:** yes
- **Added:** 2026-08-06
- **Came from:** The firm's proposal rules
- **Detail:** [`proposal-generator` skill](../../../.claude/skills/proposal-generator/)

**What happened.** Preparing a P&L for a client with no books is real work that has nothing to do
with preparing the return, and it kept getting absorbed into the tax-prep fee.

**The rule.** It's quoted separately and **never appears in the proposal at all** — it goes out as
its own invoice, with no proposal attached. Related: every proposal carries a **"Not Included"**
section whose standing defaults are **personal tax preparation** and **historical cleanup**.
Naming what's excluded up front is what makes the separate invoice a non-argument later.
