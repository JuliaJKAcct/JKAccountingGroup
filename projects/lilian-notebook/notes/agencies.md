# Agencies & filings

Government agencies and the filings we make to them — how they actually behave,
what they gate on, and what to get in writing.

## LN-01 — Register a state payroll account as late as you can, and pin down the quarter the provider starts filing
- **Tags:** payroll · florida · registration · providers
- **Certainty:** Established
- **Star:** yes
- **Added:** 2026-08-06
- **Came from:** Tsminibears LLC — Florida reemployment-tax penalties
- **Detail:** [`tsminibears.md` §5](../../client-intelligence/clients/tsminibears.md) · Double case note 485230

**What happened.** The firm registered the Florida RT account, then engaged Gusto — not
knowing Gusto files nothing until the first payroll runs. Q2 and Q3 2025 belonged to
nobody. Result: penalties of roughly $842.

**The rule.** Registering the state account starts the clock, whether or not wages are
ever paid. So register it in the quarter payroll actually starts — never later than the
quarter wages are first paid, which creates the opposite exposure — and the moment a
provider is engaged, **ask it in writing which quarter it begins filing from.** Diarise
the $0 returns for every quarter before that date, and treat any client with a
registered account and no payroll yet as exposed.

## LN-02 — If one representative starts listing requirements, call again. But a friendlier verbal answer is still verbal
- **Tags:** phone calls · evidence · florida
- **Certainty:** Established
- **Star:** yes
- **Added:** 2026-08-06
- **Updated:** 2026-08-06
- **Came from:** Three Florida DOR calls in three days, 2026-08-04 → 2026-08-06
- **Detail:** [`tsminibears.md` §4/§5](../../client-intelligence/clients/tsminibears.md)

**What happened.** Three calls, three pictures. **4 Aug:** close the account retroactively and
all penalties go away — entity type never mentioned. **6 Aug:** the entity's federal filing form
gates everything, and both fixes need an IRS letter proving no salary requirement. **6 Aug, a
third agent:** the effective-date change *can* be made — no requirements raised, the letter never
mentioned, just wait for the POA. Lilian's own words: *"every agent is different, which in this
case was good."* No representative's name or ID was captured on any of the three.

**The rule.** Don't treat the first answer as the agency's position, and **when one
representative starts listing requirements before accepting a request, call again — another may
simply do it.** Then hold the caveats, because they're what stops this from becoming wishful
thinking: a friendlier answer is **still verbal**, and whoever actually processes the request can
apply the stricter rule; the third agent was **never asked** about the letter, so his silence
resolves nothing; and none of the three had a POA on file, so all of it was general information,
not an answer about the account. So: **take the name and ID every single time**, ask the
questions the representative hasn't thought to raise, and **get the acceptance in writing.** The
call is for finding out; the letter is for requesting.

## LN-33 — Verify an email address an agent reads you over the phone — then confirm it was delivered
- **Tags:** email · evidence · agencies · gmail
- **Certainty:** Established
- **Star:** yes
- **Added:** 2026-08-06
- **Came from:** Tsminibears — the POA that never arrived
- **Detail:** [`tsminibears.md` §4 "The POA that never arrived"](../../client-intelligence/clients/tsminibears.md)

**What happened.** The power of attorney was emailed to the Florida DOR on 4 Aug, to the address
the representative gave on the call. That address was wrong — *"no era ni parecido"*, not even
close. The message **bounced**, and the non-delivery notice sat unread in Gmail's **"Updates"**
tab. For two days the whole plan was waiting on a 3–4 day clock that had never started, on a
document the agency never received. Found and re-sent 6 Aug, so everything moved back three
weeks.

**The rule.** Three habits, and they take a minute: **read the address back** to the agent
character by character, or cross-check it against the agency's published contact page; **after
sending, confirm delivery** — go and look for a bounce, including in Gmail's **Updates** and spam
tabs, which is exactly where this one hid; and **don't start a clock on an unverified send** —
a deadline you're counting from a message nobody received is not running. Same for the reverse:
when the correct address is finally established, **write it down in the client file**, because
the one that worked is the thing nobody records.

## LN-03 — A state-agency matter like this runs a month, not a week: POA 3–4 days, then the change 3–4 weeks
- **Tags:** florida · poa · planning · timelines
- **Certainty:** Established
- **Star:** yes
- **Added:** 2026-08-06
- **Updated:** 2026-08-06
- **Came from:** Florida DOR, the 2026-08-04 and third 2026-08-06 calls
- **Detail:** [`tsminibears.md` §4](../../client-intelligence/clients/tsminibears.md)

**What happened.** The agency won't take any instruction on an account until the power of
attorney has processed — **3–4 days.** Then the third representative put the effective-date change
itself at a further **3–4 weeks** (his figure, still verbal). And because the first POA send never
arrived (LN-33), the clock only really started two days later. A matter everyone expected to close
that week now lands in September.

**The rule.** Send the POA **first**, and quote the client the whole chain, not the next step:
POA processing, then the change. The first call is for scoping — **the substantive call is the
second one**, days later. Anything a representative tells you before the POA is on file is
general information, not an answer about the account, so treat figures from those calls as an
order of magnitude only.

## LN-04 — A presumption is rebutted with facts, not denied. And never write "there was no obligation to file"
- **Tags:** letters · argument · penalties
- **Certainty:** Established
- **Star:** yes
- **Added:** 2026-08-06
- **Came from:** The Florida DOR effective-date request
- **Detail:** [`tsminibears.md` §4](../../client-intelligence/clients/tsminibears.md)

**What happened.** A Florida DOR representative said the agency presumes a company filing as a
corporation pays a salary, and on that basis gated both the account closure and the
effective-date change on an IRS letter showing a form with no salary requirement. Read as a
refusal, that closes the route to any S-corp. It isn't a refusal — it's a presumption. *(And it
was **one representative's** requirement, not established agency policy: a third agent the same
day raised no documentation at all — LN-02. Don't carry it to another client as settled.)*

**The rule.** An entity that genuinely files as a corporation but never paid wages
**rebuts the presumption with evidence rather than denying it**: *the company is an S-corp,
no wages were ever paid in any period, here is the proof.* Two things never go in the
letter: **"there was no obligation to file"** (the notice itself proves the agency
disagrees — the same facts win as a reasonable-cause waiver instead), and **blame aimed at
a payroll provider** (a provider is the taxpayer's own agent, so blame transfers nothing —
but *"we had engaged and were paying a provider we understood to be handling these
filings"* works as reasonable cause).

## LN-05 — Don't extract a penalty total by phone. It's on the filing screen
- **Tags:** florida · penalties · costs
- **Certainty:** Established
- **Star:** no
- **Added:** 2026-08-06
- **Came from:** Florida DOR via Lilian, 2026-08-06
- **Detail:** [`tsminibears.md` §5](../../client-intelligence/clients/tsminibears.md)

**What happened.** The representative's ≈$842.50 was expressly an estimate — the $25/month
keeps accruing, so the real figure depends on the day the returns are submitted. Worse: it later
turned out **no POA was on file** when that number was given, so it didn't come from the account
at all (LN-33).

**The rule.** Each delinquent return shows **its own cost when you go to submit it** on the
agency's website. That gives the real per-return amounts *and* the true list of outstanding
periods in one place, which the phone never does reliably.

## LN-06 — A CP575 records the filing requirement at EIN issuance, not today
- **Tags:** irs · cp575 · s-corp · evidence
- **Certainty:** Established
- **Star:** yes
- **Added:** 2026-08-06
- **Came from:** Firm analysis on Tsminibears, Aug 2026
- **Detail:** [`tsminibears.md` §5](../../client-intelligence/clients/tsminibears.md)

**What happened.** The plan was to hand Florida the client's CP575 as proof of how the
company files. But an entity that later elected S-corp treatment (Form **2553**, acknowledged
on a **CP261**) can hold a CP575 that still says *disregarded* — while the firm itself filed
an **1120-S** for it.

**The rule.** Check for a **2553 / CP261 before using a CP575 as evidence** of how a company
files. Handing a state agency a letter that contradicts the return you filed is a real
exposure, and by omission it's misrepresentation. Naming note: the house calls the CP575
"the SS-4" because the CP575 cites Form SS-4 on its face — the SS-4 is the *application*,
the CP575 is what the IRS sends back, and a **147C** is the replacement when it's lost.

## LN-07 — Don't put a form number in an agency's mouth
- **Tags:** evidence · record-keeping · irs
- **Certainty:** Established
- **Star:** no
- **Added:** 2026-08-06
- **Came from:** Lilian, 2026-08-06 — *"ella no me dijo el nombre, yo fui la que estaba asumiendo"*
- **Detail:** [`tsminibears.md` §5](../../client-intelligence/clients/tsminibears.md)

**What happened.** The DOR asked for "a letter from the IRS stating how the company files
its taxes" and never named a form. Identifying it as the CP575 was the firm's own step — a
sound one, but ours.

**The rule.** Record a requirement **the way the agency stated it**, and keep your own
identification of the document visibly separate. A form number attributed to an agency that
never said it reads as settled policy six weeks later, and then a plan gets built on it.

## LN-08 — Before recommending you close a state account, ask what the client loses by not having it
- **Tags:** payroll · s-corp · advice · florida
- **Certainty:** Established
- **Star:** no
- **Added:** 2026-08-06
- **Came from:** Julia, Aug 2026 — she refused the closure
- **Detail:** [`tsminibears.md` §5](../../client-intelligence/clients/tsminibears.md)

**What happened.** Closing the RT account retroactively looked like the cheapest fix. Julia
refused it: an S-corp protecting its status may have to run payroll, payroll needs an open RT
account, and reopening the same number is only a **request**.

**The rule.** Where two fixes both clear the problem, prefer the one that **keeps the account
open** — moving the effective date beats closing it. Don't optimise for the smaller invoice by
default, and let whoever owns the relationship make that call.

## LN-09 — A Form 8802 that goes quiet is usually waiting on the underlying returns
- **Tags:** irs · form-8802 · residency · fax
- **Certainty:** Working assumption
- **Star:** no
- **Added:** 2026-08-06
- **Came from:** Ihor Naum — U.S. residency certification, filed 2026-05-06
- **Detail:** [`ihor-naum-olha-levchuk.md` §4/§5](../../client-intelligence/clients/ihor-naum-olha-levchuk.md)

**What happened.** Filed for Ukraine, years 2023–2024, in the individual's own name. Fee paid
on Pay.gov, then **faxed twice about 25 minutes apart**. Twelve weeks later: nothing.

**The rule.** Two things to check before chasing: **were the years' returns actually on file
at transmission** (the firm's working theory for why an 8802 stalls — plausible, not
confirmed), and **did the double transmission create a duplicate** that the IRS is now
holding. Also: confirm an **individual** Form 2848 exists, or the firm can't call about it at
all. Status line: 267-941-1000, "U.S. residency" option.
