# Part 1 — The rules we follow

## Rule 1 · The personal brand carrying this firm is **Julia's**

The source's central claim holds for us: people don't follow a logo, and in a
boutique advisory firm they don't buy a service — they buy **the person they will
be dealing with**. Our positioning already says the differentiator is "a senior
advisor in your corner." That advisor has a name.

So: posts are written from **Julia** (Chief Accountant — never "CEO"), in first
person, and she appears in the visual whenever the format allows a face. The firm
account is where Julia is visible, not a corporate noticeboard. The team (Lilian,
Maria) appears in its own right — a firm of real people is proof, not a dilution.

What this does **not** mean: it is still JK Accounting Group's account, on JK's
palette and type, under the brand guide's voice.

## Rule 2 · Positioning must land on someone seeing the profile for the first time

The source's test: *your positioning must be understandable to a person who has
just landed on your profile.* Four elements have to be answerable at a glance —
**who you are · who you help · what problem you solve · why you.**

Filled for JK, straight from [`positioning.md`](./positioning.md):

| Element | JK Accounting Group |
|---|---|
| **Who we are** | A boutique, founder-led US accounting firm — Miami · Fort Lauderdale · online — led by Julia, Chief Accountant. Bicultural: US rules, your language. |
| **Who we help** | Foreign business owners operating or expanding in the US — primarily Ukrainian- and Russian-speaking. |
| **What problem we solve** | They don't know the rules here yet. Bookkeeping happens reactively (or through a home-country bookkeeper who doesn't know the US system), so tax time is a scramble and there is nobody senior to ask when a real decision comes up. |
| **Why us** | We become their finance department year-round: correct bookkeeping from day one, quarterly planning check-ins, and direct access to a senior advisor who speaks their language — never a call center, never alone in a foreign system. |

**The one-line formula** (the source's *"I help [whom] get [result] through [your
approach]"*), in the firm's own voice:

> **EN —** We help foreign business owners in the US stop guessing at the American
> system and keep more of what they earn — by becoming their finance department
> year-round, in their own language.

> **RU —** Мы помогаем иностранным предпринимателям в США перестать угадывать
> американские правила и оставлять себе больше заработанного — становясь их
> финансовым отделом на весь год и говоря с ними на их языке.

Use this to write the bio, the first line of a carousel, and the "who this is for"
line of any offer post. It is a **derivative** of `positioning.md`, not a second
source of truth — if the two ever disagree, `positioning.md` wins and this table
gets corrected.

## Rule 3 · Every post stands on one of three pillars

The source's model, adopted as-is. A post that belongs to none of the three is
not published.

| Pillar | What it does | What it looks like for an accounting firm |
|---|---|---|
| **1 · Expertise** | Attracts attention | Deadline explainers · "what an S-corp actually saves, with the arithmetic" · how a US bank/IRS notice/1099 actually works · answers to the questions clients really ask · a professional opinion on a change in the rules · walkthroughs (how to read your P&L, what we need for a return) |
| **2 · Personality** | Creates the emotional connection | Julia's own path — arriving, building a practice here · why the firm exists · what we refuse to do and why · how the team works · the difficulties and what they taught us · what we believe about money and business |
| **3 · Proof** | Builds trust, and therefore sales | Before / after (books in chaos → books that close monthly) · what changed for a client · the firm's verifiable track record · our own progress — new services, the team growing, a tool we built |

The source's summary is worth keeping verbatim: *expertise attracts attention,
personality creates the connection, results create trust and sales.* One pillar
alone underperforms — expertise without personality is a textbook, personality
without proof is a diary.

## Rule 4 · The monthly mix is 50 / 20 / 20 / 10

Not per post — **per month**, checked when the plan is built.

| Share | Bucket | On a 14-post month |
|---|---|---|
| **50%** | **Useful** — teach, explain, hand over something usable | ~7 posts |
| **20%** | **Personality** — the human behind the expert | ~3 posts |
| **20%** | **Trust / proof** — cases, results, testimonials, behind the scenes | ~3 posts |
| **10%** | **Offer** — the service, the consultation, working with us | **1–2 posts** |

The 10% is the point of the other 90%, and it is **not optional**: sell softly,
but sell **regularly**. A month with no offer post is a month that taught the
audience we don't want the work.

When someone asks for "a post", say which bucket it fills and what the month's mix
looks like so far. That is the reminder this playbook exists to give.

## Rule 5 · One post = one action, named before it is written

The source's pre-publication question — *what action should the person take after
seeing this?* — is our hardest content rule, because it is the one that separates
activity from growth: **content without a goal creates activity; content with a
strategy creates growth.**

Pick exactly one, before the first line is written:

- **Save it** (reference material — deadlines, checklists, "what to bring us")
- **Follow** (a series, a promise of more)
- **Comment** (a genuine question we will actually answer)
- **Click the link** (a lead magnet, an article, the site)
- **Book a free discovery call** — **the wording is fixed**: *free discovery call*,
  never *free consultation*. A consultation is paid advisory work ($150 / 1 hour);
  the discovery call is free, 10 minutes, by phone. See
  [`positioning.md`](./positioning.md), and **check the booking link resolves
  before it goes into anything published.**

A post whose action you cannot name in one word is not finished.

## Rule 6 · Consistency beats perfection — 3–4 posts a week, planned

Straight from the source, and it is the rule most firms break: *regularity is more
important than perfection — 3–4 quality posts a week to a strategy beat a chaotic
stream with no system.* A burst of nine posts followed by five silent weeks costs
more reach than it earns.

Plan a month at a time (topic · pillar · bucket · action), then produce against
the plan. Recognition is not an accident — it is the output of a system run daily.

## Rule 7 · Proof, without breaking client confidentiality

**This is ours, not the source's** — and it is the one place a personal-brand kit
written for the creator economy cannot be followed literally. The source's "Result"
pillar assumes you can publish client names, screenshots and numbers. We are an
accounting firm; our clients' figures are their private financial data.

So, for anything in the Proof pillar:

- **No client name, business name, logo, or identifying detail** without their
  explicit written permission — asked for as a permission, never assumed from a
  friendly relationship.
- **No client figures.** Not a screenshot of their books, not their refund, not
  their revenue. A pattern is publishable; *their* number is not.
- **Anonymize to the situation, not the person:** "an owner who had been filing as
  a sole proprietor for three years while running payroll-sized profits" — the
  shape of the case, which is what actually teaches.
- **Real facts only.** Every figure, threshold, deadline or statistic is verified
  against an authoritative source (IRS first) before it is published; if it can't
  be verified, it is cut. No invented stats, no fabricated testimonials, no stock
  or AI faces. *(Brand guide, §2.)*
- **Educational, not advice.** Tax content is general education, not advice on a
  reader's situation — keep that line visible in posts that could be read as a
  recommendation.

## Rule 8 · The voice does not change because it is social

Social rewards volume and heat; we don't pay that price. The brand guide governs
here without exception: **a calm senior advisor — never fear-based tax marketing,
never hype, never hard-sell.** Our audience is already anxious; we are the calm in
the room. "Viral" for us means *clear, useful and shareable*, not clickbait.

Where the source is louder than we are — *monetization*, *influence*, *financial
freedom*, *become a magnet* — take the mechanism and drop the register. See Part 3.

## Rule 9 · Bilingual EN / RU, each written natively

Per the brand guide: every language is written natively, never
machine-translated — match the quality, not the word order. Ukrainian when the
piece calls for it. This applies to the caption, the on-image text and the CTA
alike.

## Before publishing — the checklist

1. **Pillar** named (expertise · personality · proof) and it holds.
2. **Bucket** counted against this month's 50/20/20/10.
3. **One action** named, and the post actually asks for it.
4. **Positioning** legible to a first-time visitor — no insider shorthand.
5. **Facts verified**; no unverifiable number survived.
6. **No client data** — no name, no figures, no identifying detail without written permission.
7. **Voice** — calm advisor; no fear, no hype, no hard-sell.
8. **Bilingual** EN/RU, each written natively.
9. **Brand** — palette, type, logo per the brand guide; **exact text placed by us, never typed by an image model** (`collateral/social-post-workflow.md`).
10. **Right size** for the network, and any link **opens** before it goes out.

# Part 2 — The source, digested

**Ekaterina Odintsova — *Personal Brand Starter Kit: 5 шагов к узнаваемости и
монетизации* ("Личный бренд, который работает на вас"), Creator Season.** A
six-page mini-guide to building a personal brand and income through social media.
Brought by Julia, August 2026. Digested here so the content is not lost with the
session; the original PDF stays with Julia.

**Cover — the five promises.** Recognition (*узнаваемость*) · expertise
(*экспертность*) · monetization (*монетизация*) · growth (*рост*) · influence
(*влияние*).

**Slide 2 — The strong-positioning formula.** Positioning must combine four
elements: **who you are** (experience, role, professional direction) · **whom you
help** (a concrete audience you actually want) · **what problem you solve** (the
result the person gets because of you) · **why you** (your approach, story,
values, or unique method). Fill the formula: *"I help [whom] get [result] through
[my approach]."* Their worked example: *"I help women experts package their
personal brand, become more visible, and start monetizing their knowledge through
social media."* **The test: your positioning must be understandable even to
someone who has just seen your profile for the first time.**

**Slide 3 — The three pillars of a personal brand.** For the audience to remember
you, content must regularly open up three directions: **① Expertise** — show what
you know and can do: instructions, breakdowns, cases, answers to questions,
professional opinion, results of clients and students. **② Personality** — let
people get to know you: your story, values, principles, way of life, difficulties
and conclusions, what you believe in. **③ Result** — show the transformation you
create: before and after, testimonials, achievements, changes in clients, your own
path and progress. *"Expertise attracts attention. Personality creates the
emotional connection. Results form trust and sales."*

**Slide 4 — Content that grows a personal brand.** A simple content model:
**50% useful** (teach, explain, share tools and solutions — expert content that
helps your audience) · **20% personality** (your path, values, views, important
events — show the person behind the expert) · **20% trust** (cases, testimonials,
results, the work process, behind the scenes — evidence of your effectiveness) ·
**10% sale** (products, services, consultations, opportunities to work with you —
make the offer softly, but regularly). **Before every publication ask yourself:
what action should the person take after seeing this?** — save · subscribe ·
comment · click the link · book a consultation. **Important: regularity beats
perfection** — 3–4 quality publications a week to a strategy beat a chaotic stream
of content with no system or goal. What the approach yields: steady growth in
reach and engagement, an emotional connection through your personality and values,
a high level of trust from results and testimonials, a clear path to purchase, and
sustainable brand development that attracts opportunities, partnerships and
clients. *"Content without a goal creates activity. Content with a strategy
creates growth."*

**Slide 5 — The system that produces the result.** Recognition and monetization
are not accidents; they are the output of a system, a strategy and daily action.
Five stages: **① Strategy** — define your path, positioning and target audience; a
clear plan built on your strengths. **② Content** — expert, engaging content that
forms your image, transmits your values and attracts the right people.
**③ Audience** — attract and warm the audience through meaning, usefulness and
trust; build a community around the brand. **④ Monetization** — package knowledge
and experience into products, services and collaborations that produce stable
income. **⑤ Scaling** — optimize the processes and build a system that lets you
grow without losing yourself or your quality. What you get: a strong personal
brand that stands out and is remembered; a loyal audience that trusts and
recommends; a systematic flow of clients and opportunities; confidence, freedom
and financial growth; influence and recognition in your niche. *"A personal brand
is your capital. The system is your growth instrument. Act consistently — the
result is inevitable."*

**Slide 6 — The result: new opportunities and financial freedom.** What you end
up with: **recognition and respect** (you are known, trusted, your opinion is
listened to) · **new opportunities** (invitations to collaborations, interviews,
events, projects and partnerships) · **stable income** (monetization through
products, services, consultations, collaborations and your own projects) ·
**freedom and choice** (you decide who to work with and how to build your life) ·
**real fulfilment** (doing what you love, inspiring people, creating value). You
become a magnet for ideal clients, advantageous offers and collaborations, media
and event invitations, investment and partnership opportunities, and a loyal
engaged audience. *"Remember: people don't buy only a product or a service. They
buy your story, your values and your vision."* · *"A personal brand is not just
about popularity. It is about influence, trust, and the freedom to create the life
you want."* · **System + strategy + consistency = an inevitable result.**

# Part 3 — What we took, adapted, and left

Being explicit about this is the point: the source is a personal-brand kit written
for the creator economy, and we are a regulated professional practice serving an
anxious audience.

| The source says | What we did |
|---|---|
| Positioning formula, four elements, understandable at first glance | **Taken whole** (Rule 2) — filled from `positioning.md` |
| Three pillars: expertise · personality · result | **Taken whole** (Rule 3) — examples made accounting-specific |
| 50 / 20 / 20 / 10 content mix | **Taken whole** (Rule 4) — measured monthly, ~14 posts |
| One action per publication, decided in advance | **Taken whole** (Rule 5) — CTA list bound to our fixed "free discovery call" wording |
| Regularity beats perfection, 3–4 posts a week | **Taken whole** (Rule 6) |
| The five-stage system (strategy → content → audience → monetization → scaling) | **Taken as the frame**, not as a service to buy. Our stages already exist across the marketing projects — the funnel in [`referral-offer-strategy/`](./referral-offer-strategy/), the entry points in [`lead-magnets/`](./lead-magnets/), the booking front door in [`consultation-booking/`](./consultation-booking/) |
| Publish client results, testimonials, before/after | **Adapted, hard** (Rule 7) — an accounting firm cannot publish client data. Anonymized patterns, verified facts, written permission or nothing |
| The personal brand is the individual expert | **Adapted** (Rule 1) — it is Julia, inside JK's brand, with the team visible too |
| Register: *monetization · influence · become a magnet · financial freedom* | **Left** — the mechanism is right, the register is not ours. The brand guide bans hype and fear-based marketing; we are the calm in the room (Rule 8) |
| "5 steps to recognition and monetization" as a program to buy | **Not adopted** — this is a lead magnet for the author's own offer. We took the content, not the funnel |
