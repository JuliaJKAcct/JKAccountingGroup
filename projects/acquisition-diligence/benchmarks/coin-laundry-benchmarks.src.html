Florida Coin Laundry Benchmarks

The yardstick for reading a laundromat's numbers: what the cost structure should look like, which KPIs decide the deal, and the three independent ways to test whether reported revenue is real.

Compiled 29 Aug 2026 Markets South Florida · Tampa Bay–Sarasota Basis Published industry + utility data
Read this before using any number below

"South Florida" and the Bradenton–Palmetto–Ruskin corridor are not one market

Those towns sit in Manatee and Hillsborough counties — the Tampa Bay–Sarasota metro, roughly 200 miles from Miami. That distinction is not pedantry here, because the single largest controllable cost in a laundromat is water and sewer, and it is set county by county.

On the published schedules, high-volume commercial water + sewer runs about $7.49 per 1,000 gallons in unincorporated Manatee County against roughly $25 per 1,000 gallons in Miami-Dade's upper tier — on the order of three times the cost for the same wash. Benchmarked against South Florida utility costs, a Bradenton store's utility line looks impossibly low; benchmarked the other way, a Miami store looks broken. Both readings would be wrong. Everything below is therefore given for both markets.

Where the money goes

The cost structure, as a share of gross revenue

Laundromats are a utility-and-occupancy business with a thin labor layer — not a labor business. Margin is won or lost on two lines, and both are visible from outside the company.

Darker segment = the range most stores land in. Lighter extension = the tail that still occurs, with a reason attached. Percentages are of gross revenue.

Utilities — the swing factor

20–30%

Modern high-efficiency equipment can hold this near 15%. Equipment past its service life pushes it to 35–40%. A store reporting under 15% in a high-rate county is either revenue-overstated or paying some utilities off-book.

Owner earnings (SDE)

20–35%

The band well-run stores occupy. Reaching it depends far more on rent and utility rates than on operating skill — which is why the same store is a good buy on one side of a county line and not the other.

Revenue test 1

The water method, made computable

Water is the one input a laundromat cannot fake: it arrives through a metered pipe and a third party bills for it. Every wash consumes a knowable quantity, so the utility bill sets a physical ceiling on how much washing can possibly have happened — and therefore on revenue.

Work it backwards from the bill. Net the water down to what the washers actually used, convert gallons into cycles, price the cycles, and add the dryer share. The result is what the store could have earned. Compare that with what it says it earned.

Reported materially above water-implied

The physical ceiling was exceeded. Revenue is overstated, the machine or vend assumptions are wrong, or water is being bought on a meter you haven't been shown. This is the finding the test exists to produce.

Reported materially below water-implied

Not automatically good news. It commonly means cash leaving before it is recorded — so the buyer cannot pay for it, cannot finance against it, and inherits the tax exposure attached to it.

A pro-forma item that is easy to miss

Florida repealed sales tax on commercial rent — effective 1 October 2025

Florida was the only state that taxed business rent. The tax had already been stepped down to 2.0% before being repealed outright by the elimination of §212.031; local discretionary surtaxes on rent went with it.

The consequence for a deal reviewed in 2026: any financials covering periods before October 2025 carry a rent-tax line that will not recur. On a leased location that is a real, defensible, forward-looking adjustment — and one a seller working from historical statements may not have made. On an owned location it is irrelevant, which is one more reason the real estate and the operating business have to be modelled apart.

Pricing

What the earnings are worth

Laundromats trade on earnings, not revenue. A revenue multiple is a sanity check on the answer — never the method that produces it.

BasisRangeWhen it applies
SDE multiple — single store3.0–5.0×The standard basis for a single Florida laundromat. Marketplace data across 855 sales (2021–2025) shows an average of 3.65× and a median of 3.50×, with 2025 the strongest reading in that window at 4.12×.
EBITDA multiple — multi-store4.0–6.0×Multi-location groups are normally priced on EBITDA rather than SDE, because no single owner's labor is being added back across five sites.
Revenue multiple0.3–0.5×Cross-check only. A revenue multiple cannot distinguish a store with 22% utilities from one with 38%.
Real estateSeparateValued on its own cap rate and appraised independently. Do not let the business multiple absorb the property, or the buyer pays an earnings multiple for land.

Florida market context

~$949,500

Median asking price for an established Florida laundromat, against a range running from under $162,500 to well past $1.16M. Asking prices — not transaction prices.

What moves the multiple

Equipment age, lease term, card-system maturity and whether real estate is included account for swings above 25%. A store at the top of the range and one at the bottom can post identical revenue.

What a benchmark can and cannot do

Every range on this page is a screen. It tells you whether a reported number is plausible and where to point the next question — it is not evidence about this business. The evidence is the metered bill, the processor settlement, the bank statement and the IRS transcript. Where a benchmark and a document disagree, the document governs and the benchmark becomes the reason you looked.
