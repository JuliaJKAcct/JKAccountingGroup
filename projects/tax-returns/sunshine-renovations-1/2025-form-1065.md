## 0 · The year in one paragraph

The company bought a house on **19 July 2024**, renovated it, and sold it on **13 June 2025**. It is
a **dealer**: the property was acquired for resale in the ordinary course, was **inventory** under
§1221(a)(1), was **never placed in service**, and no depreciation was ever claimed. The 2024 return
capitalised everything onto the balance sheet and **reported zero on every line of page 1** — so the
whole of 2024's cost, carrying cost and error lands in 2025. **2024 is taken exactly as filed; no
amended return.** The 2025 return therefore has one job: move a balance sheet of **402,796 of
non-cash assets, 304,759 of liabilities and 101,100 of capital** to a balance sheet holding
**22,100.79 of cash and nothing else**. Every figure below is forced by that, and all of it now
reconciles — 11 independent ties, no plugs, one 0.08 rounding difference that is itself explained.

The year produced an **ordinary business loss of (25,882.21)**.

## 4 · Decisions taken, and the alternative

**① The property is INVENTORY, not a depreciable asset.** Julia. The alternative — the prior year's
own presentation on Schedule L lines 9a/11 — is not available: the company bought, renovated and
resold inside eleven months and never rented it, so it was never placed in service. *"Never placed
in service" is the wrong reason to give for no depreciation, though* — the right one is that
**inventory is never depreciable** (Reg. §1.167(a)-2).

**② 2024 is taken exactly as filed — no amendment.** Julia, 2026-09-14: *"We do have to account for
2024 SRS because we're not going to do any amendments, so take the same numbers."* The alternative
was amending 2024 to move the carrying costs and correct the duplication. Consequence: **three
prior-year errors are corrected through the 2025 return** and disclosed in the statement at §9.

**③ Carrying costs capitalised in 2024 are charged to income in 2025, not treated as cost of goods
sold.** Julia, 2026-09-14: *"I still think there are items that are added to the basis, and there
are items that are an actual operating expense, like taxes, licenses, and interest."* They go on
their natural lines (14, 15, 20). The alternative — folding them into inventory — would put interest
and property tax into COGS, which is what made the first draft's COGS smaller than the opening
inventory and started this whole reconstruction.

**④ The 3,550 is BUILDER'S RISK INSURANCE, paid to the insurance company.** Julia, 2026-09-14:
*"It wasn't an HOA. It's not correctly written. It was builder risk insurance charged. I put it as
escrow closing out cost, but they were actually paid to the insurance company."* The 2024 HUD Tool
had booked it as *Lender Reserve Escrow (Current Asset)* and the label stuck. Corroborated: FCI's
March **and** June 2025 statements both show an escrow balance of **0.00**, so nothing was ever held.
⚠️ Julia has asked for the client to confirm the premium was actually **paid**.

**⑤ The 11,500 received in April and May is a LOAN, not a contribution.** Julia, 2026-09-14. It came
from a third party (not a partner), and **11,500 of the 22,500 paid to that person in July is its
repayment**. The **remaining 11,000 settled a loan the same person had made to Vitaliy personally**,
so the company paying it is a **distribution to Vitaliy**. Effect: contributions fall 18,740 →
**7,240** and distributions 71,857 → **60,357**; both move by the same 11,500, so **the loss does not
change**, but Schedule K line 19a, Schedule M-2 lines 2a and 6a, and both item L blocks do.

**⑥ The 23 June 25,000 is treated as a DISTRIBUTION, not wages.** 🔴 **The largest open item on the
return** — see §6. Chase labels it *"Online Realtime Payroll Payment"*, which is the name of the
payment rail, not a classification. Two facts support distribution: the partnership has **never
filed a Form 941 and never issued a W-2**, and the payment is **ten days after the sale closed**.
If it is wages the loss becomes **(50,882.21)** — a 25,000 swing.

**⑦ 6,376.75 of construction facility that was never drawn, and 1,564 of unidentified liabilities,
are written off as a REDUCTION OF 2025 DEDUCTIONS.** Neither was ever owed, so nothing was
discharged and this is not §61(a)(11) income — it is the correction of a prior-year balance-sheet
overstatement, disclosed in the statement.

**⑧ The FCI payoff is split 2,370.42 interest / 3,459.07 fees** using the 2025 Form 1098. The
alternative is the payoff letter, which the firm does not hold. **The total is unaffected either
way** — only the split between line 15 and line 20 moves.

### ⚠️ The negative-capital exposure

Loss splits 50/50: **(12,941.11) / (12,941.10)**. Distributions do **not** — 11,000 are Vitaliy's
alone, 200 are Roman's, and 49,157 are unassigned pending ① and ②. **If both unassigned pieces turn

left to shelter it**, so the excess distribution is a **§731(a)(1) gain on his 1040**. Worth settling
**before** the K-1 goes out.

## 10 · What actually happened along the way

- **The first worksheet left 4,036.28 that would not trace**, and it was delivered saying so rather
  than plugged. Three documents closed it and **none of them needed a guess**: the FCI payment
  statement (the loan was interest-only and 6,376.75 was never drawn), the settlement statement read
  line by line (three charges were missing entirely), and the seller's wire (663.72 never arrived).
- 🔑 **The residual moved because the MODEL was wrong, not the arithmetic.** The first model assumed
  the payoff equalled the book liability, counted the 1098 interest on top of the cash that paid part
  of it, and dropped the 528.19 of seller credits. Rebuilding it as *"cash is cash and the balance
  sheet is anchored at both ends"* made the answer independent of how the payoff splits — and that
  is what made it tie.
- **Julia's own correction of the 3,550** (builder's risk, not escrow, not HOA) arrived mid-build and
  is the kind of thing no document would have revealed: the HUD Tool's bucket name had become the
  fact.
