## 4 · Decisions taken, and the alternative

**① The property is INVENTORY, not a depreciable asset.** Julia. The alternative — the prior year's
own presentation on Schedule L lines 9a/11 — is not available: the company bought, renovated and
resold inside eleven months and never rented it, so it was never placed in service. *"Never placed
in service" is the wrong reason to give for no depreciation, though* — the right one is that
**inventory is never depreciable** (Reg. §1.167(a)-2).

**② 2024 is taken exactly as filed — no amendment.** Julia, 2026-09-14: *"We do have to account for
2024 SRS because we're not going to do any amendments, so take the same numbers."* The alternative
was amending 2024 to move the carrying costs and correct the duplication. Consequence: **three
prior-year errors are corrected through the 2025 return** and disclosed in the statement at §9.

**③ Carrying costs capitalised in 2024 are charged to income in 2025, not treated as cost of goods
sold.** Julia, 2026-09-14: *"I still think there are items that are added to the basis, and there
are items that are an actual operating expense, like taxes, licenses, and interest."* They go on
their natural lines (14, 15, 20). The alternative — folding them into inventory — would put interest
and property tax into COGS, which is what made the first draft's COGS smaller than the opening
inventory and started this whole reconstruction.

**④ The 3,550 is BUILDER'S RISK INSURANCE, paid to the insurance company.** Julia, 2026-09-14:
*"It wasn't an HOA. It's not correctly written. It was builder risk insurance charged. I put it as
escrow closing out cost, but they were actually paid to the insurance company."* The 2024 HUD Tool
had booked it as *Lender Reserve Escrow (Current Asset)* and the label stuck.
✅ **CLOSED 2026-09-14, by Julia: the LENDER PAID IT AT THE 2024 CLOSING.** So it was paid, it is a
2024 cost, and it is released in 2025 on line 21 as insurance. FCI's escrow balance of **0.00** in
March and June 2025 is not a coincidence — nothing was held because it had already gone out to the
insurer.
⛔ **And it is NOT the 2025 Southwind HOA.** Both reviewers independently noticed that the 2025 HOA
is **1,775.00 × 2 = 3,550.00 exactly** and asked whether the two were one item. They are not; two
different things happen to come to the same figure. **The coincidence was worth raising and it is
now answered.**

**⑤ The 11,500 received in April and May is a LOAN, not a contribution.** Julia, 2026-09-14. It came
from a third party (not a partner), and **11,500 of the 22,500 paid to that person in July is its
repayment**. The **remaining 11,000 settled a loan the same person had made to Vitaliy personally**,
so the company paying it is a **distribution to Vitaliy**. Effect: contributions fall 18,740 →
**7,240** and distributions 71,857 → **60,357**; both move by the same 11,500, so **the loss does not
change**, but Schedule K line 19a, Schedule M-2 lines 2a and 6a, and both item L blocks do.

**⑥ The 23 June 25,000 is treated as a DISTRIBUTION, not wages.** 🔴 **The largest open item on the
return** — see §6. Chase labels it *"Online Realtime Payroll Payment"*, which is the name of the
payment rail, not a classification. Two facts support distribution: the partnership has **never
filed a Form 941 and never issued a W-2**, and the payment is **ten days after the sale closed**.
If it is wages the loss becomes **(50,882.21)** — a 25,000 swing.

**⑦ Dealer status — and it is the biggest judgement on the return.** Everything rests on it:
ordinary versus capital, Form 1125-A existing at all, self-employment income, §199A, and no Form
4797 or Schedule D. Julia's standing ruling for this group is that renovation held for sale is work
in progress and the gain is ordinary. ⚠️ **The evidence is one property, one purchase, one sale,
eleven months — and the 2024 return itself put the property on Schedule L line 9a, "Buildings and
other DEPRECIABLE assets."** 🔴 **If it fails, the loss is a SHORT-TERM capital loss** (19 Jul 2024
→ 13 Jun 2025 is under twelve months), Schedule K line 14a goes to zero, Form 1125-A comes off the
return and the sale moves to Form 8949. *The first version of this paper did not list it as a
judgement at all, which is exactly the failure the review exists to catch.*

**⑧ The FCI payoff is split 2,370.42 interest / 3,459.07 fees** using the 2025 Form 1098. The
alternative is the payoff letter, which the firm does not hold. **The total is unaffected either
way** — only the split between line 15 and line 21 moves. ⚠️ **An earlier draft explained the
3,459.07 as three late fees of 284.45. That is 853.35, so the explanation did not fit the amount

**⑨ 🔴 The duplicated 8,337 is corrected IN 2025, through Schedule M-2 line 7 rather than as a second
deduction.** *(From the independent tax review, 2026-09-14.)*

🔑 **Julia's instruction governs and is followed in full:** *"adjust any cost in 2025 if I overstated
by mistake something in 2024… Can you just make an adjustment in 2025? I'm not trying to duplicate
8,337."* **Both halves of that are honoured.** The correction happens entirely inside the 2025
return, 2024 is untouched, and the balance sheet still clears to zero. The only question the review
settled is **which 2025 line it lands on.**

The costs behind it are real and **are** deducted — once. They are the **8,336.50** the HUD Tool
folded into the 188,332 of Building, and this return deducts them on lines 14, 15 and 21. The 8,337

⚠️ **The first delivered version deducted it**, which would have been the duplication Julia was

deductions on the face of the return. 🔑 **And this is what lets Schedule B question 6 be answered

**⑪ The 663.72 withheld from the seller's wire is treated as a cost of the sale.** Both ends are

**⑫ The 2024 carrying costs go on page 1, not into beginning inventory** *(Julia, ③ above)*. The

### ⚖️ What the reconciliation proves — and what it cannot

🔴 **An earlier version of this paper said the capital accounts "independently require" the loss.
That was too strong.** Once every 2025 cash movement is classified and the 2024 balance sheet is
fully released, the profit-and-loss route and the capital route are the **same equation**; they agree
by construction. The arithmetic reviewer demonstrated it by re-splitting the cash 20,000 different
ways — **the tie held every time.**

- ✅ **What it does prove**, and it is worth a great deal: the build is **complete** and contains **no
  duplicate**. Add a cost twice or leave one out and the two sides part by exactly that amount. It is
  also a real keying check.
- ⛔ **What it cannot do:** tell a **cost** from a **distribution**. Move the 25,000 from distributions
  to deductions and both sides move together. The 25,000 and the 24,157 are **49,157 between them —
  nearly twice the reported loss** — and the reconciliation is blind to both.
- ⛔ **"Reconciled to the penny" has been withdrawn.** The 2024 return is in whole dollars, the 815 on

## 10 · Elections to make, and what is still missing from the return

### 🔴 The §448(d)(3) tax-shelter election — make it, and it is free

The whole small-business position — **§263A(i), §471(c), §163(j)(3)** and the cash method — collapses
if the partnership is a **syndicate**. The Form 1065 instructions: *"a syndicate is a partnership…
if more than 35% of the losses of such entity during the tax year are allocated to limited partners
or limited entrepreneurs."* A **limited entrepreneur** is someone who does not actively participate
in management — **and a general partner who does not manage counts**. This partnership allocates
**50% of its loss to each partner**, so if either of them is passive, it is a syndicate.

🔑 **The fix costs nothing.** The instructions, verbatim: *"The final regulations under section 448
permit a taxpayer to make an annual election to use its allocations made in the immediately preceding
tax year, instead of using the current tax year's allocation, to determine whether the taxpayer is a
syndicate under section 448(d)(3)… The election is made on the timely filed original return
(including extensions)."* (Reg. §1.448-2(b)(2)(iii)(B)(2).) **2024 allocated zero loss** — page 1 of
that return is entirely zero — so on the prior year's allocations the partnership cannot be a
syndicate. Annual, and irrevocable for the year.
