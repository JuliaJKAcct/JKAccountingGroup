## 4 · Decisions taken, and the alternative

### 4A · ⭐ THE $2,700 OF COUNTER CREDITS IS OWNER CAPITAL — the year's reportable transaction

**Decision:** treat the three Counter Credits (500 + 1,000 + 1,200 = **$2,700**) as
**contributions from the foreign owner**, describe them on the Part V attached statement, and
check the Part V box.

**Why, and the evidence chain:**

1. A **Counter Credit** is a deposit made at a teller window. It names no payer, so the statement
   alone never proves who made it — which is why this is written as a decision to confirm, not a
   fact read off a document.
2. There is **no revenue anywhere**: no ACH from a customer, no card settlement, no invoice
   deposit, in the account's entire life. `Gross receipts` on the 1120 is blank and total assets
   are zero.
3. Each deposit **lands 0–1 days before a payment it almost exactly funds** — 1,000 on 13 Nov
   against 850 on 14 Nov; 1,200 on 15 Dec against 1,199 the same day. That is an owner topping
   the company up to clear a specific bill, not trade.
4. The company was **80 days old before it had a bank account at all** (formed 8 Aug, account
   opened 27 Oct), so it had already been paid for by somebody.

**The alternative** — that a customer paid cash over the counter — is inconsistent with a company
with no product, no sales and no assets, and would create revenue the return does not report.
⚠️ **Ask the client to confirm it was his own money** before the statement is written.

**The authority:** Form 5472 (Rev. 12-2023) Part V — *"any other transaction as defined by
Regulations section 1.482-1(i)(7), such as amounts paid or received in connection with the
formation, dissolution, acquisition, and disposition of the entity, **including contributions to
and distributions from the entity**"*; Reg. §1.6038A-2(b)(3)(xi). **TD 9796 Example 1** is the analogous fact
pattern — its owner is a foreign *corporation* contributing assets at formation, but
§301.7701-2(c)(2)(vi) says "one foreign person", so the principle carries.

### 4B · 🔴 THE FORMATION-WINDOW COSTS ARE REPORTABLE AND THE BANK STATEMENTS ARE BLIND TO THEM

**This is the finding the statements cannot give you, and it is the most certain one of the year.**
The LLC existed from **8 Aug 2025** and had no bank account until **27 Oct 2025**. Everything paid
in those eighty days — the Sunbiz filing fee, the registered agent, any fee paid to obtain the EIN
(the IRS charges nothing for one itself), and plausibly the **LUMARI trademark filing** — came out of somebody's pocket that was not the
company's. If it was the owner's, each is *"amounts paid in connection with the **formation**…
including contributions to the entity"*: the Part V language, word for word.

**So the Part V statement is not built from the bank statements alone.** It needs the owner's own
record of what he paid before the account opened. **Ask him.**

ⓘ The USPTO receipt shows the LUMARI application was handled with **Pivniak Law** as the
correspondent — the same firm the LLC paid $850 on 14 Nov. Whether the trademark application
itself was paid before or after 27 Oct is not established.

### 4C · ⚠️ THE THREE PAYMENTS OUT NEED A RELATED-PARTY TEST — an amount is not enough

Parts IV and V only capture transactions with a **related party**. Payments to genuinely
unrelated vendors are not reportable at all, however large. So each payment out turns on one
question — *is this person or company connected to the owner or his family?*

| Payee | Amount | What is known | What decides it |
|---|---|---|---|
| **PIVNIAK LAW** | 850.00 | A law firm; the correspondent on the LUMARI trademark application | Unrelated as a firm → **not** reportable as a related-party transaction. 🔴 **But see §4D** |
| **AXY COMPANY LLC** | 600.00 | Paid by **Bank of America internal book transfer** — both accounts at the same bank — with an invoice reference (*INV No 3546*) | The invoice reference reads like a real vendor. **Is AXY connected to him or his family?** |
| **OXANA MARTOX LLC** | 1,199.00 | An LLC carrying a **personal name** | Same question — and a personal-name LLC is the shape a related party usually takes. **Ask** |

🔴 **And if either IS related, the mechanics are NOT "add it to Part IV" — that is the trap.**
Both are **U.S.** LLCs, and the instructions are explicit on all three points:

- *"Do not complete Part IV for transactions with a **domestic** related party."* Part IV is for a
  **foreign** related party; its own caution ties it to the foreign-person box in Part III.
- *"Transactions with a U.S. related party, however, are **not required to be specifically
  identified in Parts IV, V, and VI**."* So the transaction is not itemised anywhere on that form.
- *"**File a separate Form 5472 for each** foreign or U.S. person who is a related party with which
  the reporting corporation had a reportable transaction."*

**So a related AXY or Oxana Martox means an ADDITIONAL Form 5472 each** — Part III completed with
the **U.S. person** box checked, Parts IV/V/VI left blank, **line 1f not completed** (*"Do not
complete line 1f if the reportable transaction is with a U.S. related party"*), and **line 1g on
every form becomes the total number of Forms 5472 filed** — 2 or 3, not 1. That flows straight
into the rebuild spec in §4G.

### 4D · ⚠️ A PAYMENT TO AN UNRELATED PARTY CAN STILL BE A DISTRIBUTION

The related-party test in §4C is not the only one. **If the company paid a personal cost of its
owner, the transaction is a distribution to him** — reportable in Part V — whoever the money
actually went to.

ⓘ **This is the firm's analysis, not a quoted instruction, and it should be read as such.** What
supports it: Part V reaches *"any other transaction as defined by Regulations section
1.482-1(i)(7)"*, and that definition covers a transfer of money **"however such transaction is
effected"** — which reaches money the company pays on its owner's behalf as readily as money it
pays him directly. The Part V language then names *distributions from the entity* expressly. Two candidates:

- **The $850 to Pivniak Law**, *if* the legal work was the trademark **and the trademark is owned
  by Mikayel personally rather than by the LLC**. The USPTO receipt carries a line reading
  *"Place of organization / citizenship: Russian Federation"*, which fits an **individual**
  applicant — a Florida LLC's place of organization would be Florida. ⚠️ **The owner field itself
  did not survive extraction and this is NOT asserted.** It is a question, and it is cheap to
  settle: the mark's owner is public on the USPTO's TSDR database.
- **The $21 at Yellow Green Farm**, a farmers' market in Hollywood FL, on a company debit card.
  Trivial in amount, but it is the same category, and it is the kind of line that tells you how
  the card is being used generally.

### 4E · 🟠 THE CAPTION IS IN THE WRONG PLACE

### 4F · 🔴 IS THIS A FINAL RETURN? — and if not, 2026 owes one too

### 4G · 🔴 THE PREPARED 5472 CANNOT BE FILED AS IT STANDS

Part II is the reason Form 5472 exists — it is where the **foreign owner is named**, and the
instructions are explicit that *"for a foreign-owned U.S. DE, report the information for the
foreign owner on the lines provided for the 25% foreign shareholder."* On the prepared form it is
blank, as is Part III, and the owner's name appears **nowhere in the document**. Part V is
unchecked with nothing attached, and 1f/1g/1h are empty.

So the form as it stands **identifies an entity and reports nothing about anybody** — while
looking, to anyone glancing at the folder, like a finished return. Filed in this state it would
risk the §6038A penalty for a return not filed in the manner prescribed **and** leave the firm
believing the obligation was met.

**Decision: rebuild it** — but ⚠️ **confirm by eye first** that Parts II and III really are blank
on the document, since the finding above is an absence in a text extraction (§3C).

The rebuild, for the **owner's** Form 5472: Part II = the owner (name, address, country of
citizenship, FTIN or "None" — he has no U.S. identifying number to put on 4b(1) unless he holds an
ITIN); Part III = the same person as the related party, **foreign-person box checked** — which is
also what makes Part IV mandatory, by the form's own caution; **Part V
checked with the attached statement** describing the contributions (§4A) and the formation costs
(§4B); 1c from Form 1120 item D; **1f = Part IV line 22 + line 36 + the Part VI FMV + the Part V
total** (⛔ not simply "the Part V total" — see §3E); **1h = the sum of every form's 1f**;
1j (initial year) checked; Part VII lines 37–41 answered and **line 42 held until Q1 lands**.
🔴 **And assign a REFERENCE ID number** for lines 4b(2) and 8b(2) — required whenever no U.S.
identifying number goes on 4b(1)/8b(1), which is the likely branch here. The firm assigns it, it
must be the **same every year** and must never be reused for another person, so **record it in
this working paper** where next year's session will find it.

🔴 **And line 1g is not automatically 1.** It is *"the total number of Forms 5472 (including this
one) being filed for the tax year"* — so it depends on §4C. If AXY or Oxana Martox turns out to be
related, each gets **its own Form 5472** and 1g becomes 2 or 3 **on every form**. Settle §4C before
filling 1g.

### 4I · The rules this return runs on, verified 2026-09-05

Read off irs.gov the same day — **Instructions for Form 5472 (Rev. 12/2024)**, **Form 5472
(Rev. 12-2023)**, and **TD 9796** (2017-03 I.R.B.).

- **File** a pro forma Form 1120 with Form 5472 attached, by the 1120's due date including
  extensions. Only the name, address and **items B and E** need be completed.
- **"Foreign-owned U.S. DE" across the top** of the 1120 — and of the 7004.
- **Cannot be e-filed.** Fax (300 DPI or higher) to **855-887-7737**, or mail to
  *Internal Revenue Service, 1973 Rulon White Blvd, M/S 6112 Attn: PIN Unit, Ogden, UT 84201*.
  Both the return and the 7004 go there — **not** the ordinary Form 1120/7004 addresses.
- **Extension:** Form 7004, **form code 12**, same caption, same fax/address, by the regular due
  date.
- **Penalty: $25,000** for failing to file when due *and in the manner prescribed*, and the same
  for failing to keep the §1.6038A-3 records; a further **$25,000 per related party per 30-day
  period** once a failure runs 90 days past IRS notice.
- 🔴 **No relief is available, and it is worth being precise about WHICH relief, because the two
  are different things.**
  - **From FILING:** the only exception that reaches this entity is having **no reportable
    transaction in Parts IV, V and VI** (Instructions, *Exceptions from filing*). §4A and §4B are
    why it is not available here. TD 9796 also closed the **Form 5471** and **foreign sales
    corporation** exceptions to foreign-owned DEs (§1.6038A-2(e)(3) and (e)(4)), and the
    instructions add that **Exception 6 does not apply** to them either.
  - **From RECORDS:** §1.6038A-1(h) (under $10,000,000 in U.S. gross receipts) and
    §1.6038A-1(i)(1) ($5,000,000 / 10% de minimis) do **not** excuse filing — for anybody. What
    they say is that a reporting corporation meeting them *"is not subject to §§1.6038A-3 and
    1.6038A-5"*: record maintenance, and authorization of agent. **TD 9796 makes both unavailable
    to a foreign-owned U.S. DE.** Its preamble is explicit that *"the exceptions to the record
    maintenance requirements in §1.6038A-1(h) and (i) for small corporations and de minimis
    transactions would not apply to these entities."*
  - 🔴 **So this entity owes §1.6038A-3 records and has none (§2)** — and the $25,000 penalty
    above attaches to that failure independently of the Form 5472.

  ⓘ **CORRECTED 2026-09-05 in review.** An earlier draft of this section, and of the client file,
  called these two "size exceptions" and framed them as relief from *filing*. That was wrong. They
  have never excused filing; they excuse the records and agent rules. The conclusion — no relief,
  file — is unchanged, but the second $25,000 exposure only became visible once the law was stated
  correctly.

## 8 · 🔗 What flows to ANOTHER return — the handoff

**Nothing, and that is the point of a disregarded entity's pro forma filing.** The pro forma 1120
reports no income and computes no tax; the 5472 is an **information return only**. There is no
K-1, no basis schedule and no figure that lands on the owner's personal return.

**Whether the owner owes a Form 1040-NR for 2025 is a separate question with a separate answer**,
and it does not come from here — it turns on whether he had U.S.-source income, which this
company did not give him. ⚠️ Double's `Tax Return Type` property currently reads **`1040`** for a
nonresident; that is a hand-maintained column and Lilian's to correct.
