> ✅ **The rule applies to the RAW string, and to all 24 of its characters at once:**
> `3`→`O` · `[`→`w` · `R`→`n` · `I`→`e` · `V`→`r` · `R`→`n` · `E`→`a` · `Q`→`m` · `I`→`e` ·

> ⓘ *The word breaks are missing because **a space encodes to a control character** (32 − 28 = 4)
> and control characters are dropped at extraction — which is also why every other line of this
> document runs its words together.*
> ✅ **One rule, 24 characters, no residue, and the output is grammatical English.** That is why §4D
> treats it as settled rather than suggestive.
>
> ⛔ **Do NOT apply `+28` to the half-decoded string** — it yields `Owjanj]ia…`, which is what the
> second version of this paragraph inadvertently claimed as its proof.
>
> ⛔ **What the first version got wrong, because the error is instructive.** It reported *"letters
> shifted +4, symbols and digits shifted −28"* — a rule keyed on the **plaintext character class**.
> That cannot be right: it would need the same letter to take two encodings at once (`M`→`Q` and
> `M`→`1`). **The `+4` observations were an artifact of our own first decoding pass**, which
> subtracted 4 from letters only. Since **−28 then −4 is −32 — exactly the gap between lower and
> upper case** — genuinely lowercase text came back out looking like correct capitals. 🔴 **That is
> why the half-decoded document read as two-thirds sensible**, and it is the whole reason this was
> dangerous rather than merely broken.

                              ▼
⑥ Form 5472 Part IV  — lines 9–21 → total 22 ·  lines 23–35 → total 36
⑦ Form 5472 Part V   — tick the box + attach the statement  ·  Part VI if property was contributed
                              │
                              │  line 22 + line 36 + Part VI FMV + the Part V total
                              ▼
⑧ BACK TO Part I — now fill 1f, then 1g, then 1h
⑨ Form 5472 Part VII — lines 37–42  (⛔ never 43)
⑩ Assemble: Form 1120 + Form 5472 + the Part V statement → fax or mail to Ogden

and VI; 1g is *how many Forms 5472 you are filing*, which depends on §4C's answer; 1h sums 1f
across every form. **Fill page 1 top-down, skip those three, and come back.**

#### 🔄 The MIRROR SCAN — run, and it did NOT come back empty

Per the standing rule *(the [`tax-return-sop`](../../../.claude/skills/tax-return-sop/) skill §4A-M:
money that goes out and comes back is invisible when a ledger is read top to bottom, and only a sort
shows it)*. Debits and credits totalled separately, then every credit amount-matched against every
debit:

```
Operating account (Bank of America)
  8 rows = 5 out 2,700.00  ·  3 in 2,700.00  ->  NET 0.00
  no EXACT reversal: no credit equals any debit
    in   500 / 1,000 / 1,200
    out  850 / 600 / 30 / 21 / 1,199
  NOT tested: the reference re-grouping (step 3) and the mechanism naming (step 4)
```

🔑 **The scan matters here for a reason particular to this return.** A mirrored pair would not just
net an expense — **it would change the Part V figure**, because a contribution that came straight
back out is not a contribution.

> ⚠️ **CORRECTED 2026-09-06 in review — this block claimed more than it had run, which is the exact

> returned, no repeated counterparty"* and concluded *"every dollar in stayed in"*. ⛔ **Only step 2
> of the scan was run** — amount-matching — and the skill says in terms that *"amount-matching alone
> finds only the exact reversals; the reference finds the partial ones."* **Steps 3 and 4 (re-group
> on the descriptor's reference, name the mechanism) were skipped**, so every one of those negatives
> belonged to a search that never happened *([`method.md`](../../pre-return-review/method.md) rule 1b)*.
> ✅ **What the scan DID establish:** no credit equals any debit, so there is **no exact reversal**.
> **That is the whole of it.**

## 4 · Decisions taken, and the alternative

### 4A · ⭐ THE $2,700 OF COUNTER CREDITS IS OWNER CAPITAL — the year's reportable transaction

**Decision:** treat the three Counter Credits (500 + 1,000 + 1,200 = **$2,700**) as
**contributions from the foreign owner**, describe them on the Part V attached statement, and
check the Part V box.

**Why, and the evidence chain:**

1. A **Counter Credit** is a deposit made at a teller window. It names no payer, so the statement
   alone never proves who made it — which is why this is written as a decision to confirm, not a
   fact read off a document.
2. There is **no revenue anywhere**: no ACH from a customer, no card settlement, no invoice
   deposit, in the account's entire life. `Gross receipts` on the 1120 is blank and total assets
   are zero.
3. Each deposit **lands 0–1 days before a payment it almost exactly funds** — 1,000 on 13 Nov
   against 850 on 14 Nov; 1,200 on 15 Dec against 1,199 the same day. That is an owner topping
   the company up to clear a specific bill, not trade.
4. The company was **80 days old before it had a bank account at all** (formed 8 Aug, account
   opened 27 Oct), so it had already been paid for by somebody.

**The alternative** — that a customer paid cash over the counter — is inconsistent with a company
with no product, no sales and no assets, and would create revenue the return does not report.
⚠️ **Ask the client to confirm it was his own money** before the statement is written.

**The authority:** Form 5472 (Rev. 12-2023) Part V — *"any other transaction as defined by
Regulations section 1.482-1(i)(7), such as amounts paid or received in connection with the
formation, dissolution, acquisition, and disposition of the entity, **including contributions to
and distributions from the entity**"*; Reg. §1.6038A-2(b)(3)(xi). **TD 9796 Example 1** is the analogous fact
pattern — its owner is a foreign *corporation* contributing assets at formation, but
§301.7701-2(c)(2)(vi) says "one foreign person", so the principle carries.

### 4B · 🔴 THE FORMATION-WINDOW COSTS ARE REPORTABLE AND THE BANK STATEMENTS ARE BLIND TO THEM

### 4C · ⚠️ THE THREE PAYMENTS OUT NEED A RELATED-PARTY TEST — an amount is not enough

Parts IV and V only capture transactions with a **related party**. Payments to genuinely
unrelated vendors are not reportable at all, however large. So each payment out turns on one
question — *is this person or company connected to the owner or his family?*

|---|---|---|---|

    corporation** exceptions to foreign-owned DEs (§1.6038A-2(e)(3) and (e)(4)), and the

### 4J · 🔴 THE FOREIGN OWNER LIVES IN THE UNITED STATES — line 4a takes his U.S. ADDRESS

**The question, from Lilian at the keyboard, 2026-09-05:** the software would not let her pick
`United States` as the country inside the Part II line 4a address block — did she therefore have to
invent a foreign address for a man who lives in Florida?

**Answer: no, and doing it would be wrong twice over.**

**What the form and the instructions actually say — checked, not remembered:**

- **The Part II instructions carry NO instruction for line 4a at all.** They cover the heading
  checkbox, 4b(1), 4b(2), the reference ID and 4b(3) — and say only *"Report on lines 4a through 4e
  information about the direct 25% foreign shareholder…"*. **There is no address rule, and nothing
  requiring the address to be foreign.** The only "Address" instruction in the whole document is
  line **1a**, the reporting corporation's, and its *"Foreign address"* note is a **formatting**
  rule for when an address happens to be foreign.
- **The form separates WHERE HE LIVES from WHAT MAKES HIM FOREIGN, and it does it three times over.**
  Line 4a is the address; **4c** is the principal country where business is conducted, **4d** the
  country of citizenship, and **4e** the country under whose laws he files as a resident. **The
  foreignness is carried by 4c/4d/4e, never by the address.**
- **Line 4b(1) points the same way, though it does not on its own settle anything:** *"Individuals
  should enter a social security number (SSN), or an individual taxpayer identification number
  (ITIN) issued by the IRS."* **The IRS plainly contemplates a 25% foreign shareholder holding a
  U.S. tax number.** ⚠️ **It does not follow that such a person is present in the United States** —
  ITINs are routinely issued to nonresident aliens who have never set foot here (treaty claimants,
  foreign partners, foreign owners of U.S. property). *(An earlier version of this bullet said this
  line "settles it". It does not, and it sat in a list headed "checked, not remembered" — which is
  exactly the shape [`method.md`](../../pre-return-review/method.md) rule 1b exists to catch.
  **The three bullets around it carry the argument on their own.**)*
- 🔑 **And on the printed Form 5472 there is no country field on line 4a at all.** It is a single
  free address block. **The country dropdown is the SOFTWARE's, not the form's** — so this is a
  vendor constraint being mistaken for an IRS rule.

**Why the wrong answer costs something, which is why this is not a formality:**

1. **It is a false statement on a signed return.** He does not live at a foreign address.
2. 🔴 **It breaks the only channel the IRS has to reach him.** This return **cannot be e-filed** —
   it goes by fax or mail to Ogden. If the IRS writes back, that letter is the whole feedback loop,
   and a fictitious address sends it somewhere nobody reads.
3. **It would contradict the return itself.** The company's own address on Form 1120 page 1 is that
   same U.S. address, so a foreign 4a would make one return say two things.

**Living in the U.S. and being a foreign person are both true at once, and that is the entire reason
this return exists.** The F-2 exempt-individual rule means his days do not count toward substantial
presence — which is exactly what the four Forms 8843 filed in June 2026 establish, and the IRS
already holds them.

🛠️ **How to get past the software — four branches, in order, because this session has NOT seen that
input screen and will not invent a click path:**

1. **Look for a second address block or a U.S./Foreign toggle on the same shareholder record.** Most
   packages carry both and default a "foreign shareholder" into the foreign one. ✅ If it is there,
   that is the whole fix.
2. **Try leaving the country blank** and filling city, state and ZIP. Several packages read a blank
   country as domestic.
3. **Override the field.** Professional packages allow a printed-form override; ⚠️ the exact route
   is the vendor's, so use whichever your package offers.
4. ✅ **Or simply write it on the paper.** 🔑 **The usual reason to fight the software is e-file
   validation — and there is none here.** A hand-completed address block on a paper filing is not a
   defect, and it beats a wrong address every time.

🔍 **How to know it worked:** print the form and read line 4a — it shows his real U.S. address, and
it matches the address on Form 1120 page 1.
⚖️ **What it moves:** no figure at all. It moves whether the return is true, and whether the IRS can
reach him.

vendor fact and next year's version may behave differently.
