### 3F · The NOL — what the 2024 return says, and how it goes into ATX *(Lilian asked, 2026-10-08)*

**Opening NOL carryforward into 2025 = 15,778 (carried out of 2023, absorbed 0 in 2024) + 355 (arose in 2024, 2024 Form
172 line 24) = 16,133.**

- **Lilian's worry was right, and the form says why:** 2024 Form 172 line 24 (**355**) is **only the loss that arose in
  2024**. Line 23 adds back the 15,778, because *"You cannot deduct any NOL carryovers or carrybacks from other years"*
  (Instructions for Form 172, Rev. Dec 2024, line 23). So the 2023 carryover is **not inside** the 355; it is carried
  **separately**.
- **2024 absorbed none of the 15,778.** 2024 taxable income before any NOL was **−29,555** (AGI before it −355 − 29,200),
  and nothing is added back to it (no capital loss, no §1202 exclusion — Form 172 lines 17, 21, 22 are 0; no QBI deduction),
  so 2024 modified taxable income was **0** — and so was the 80% base that governs a post-2017 NOL (80% of a negative
  taxable income is nothing), so the answer is 0 on either measure — *"Your taxable income as modified cannot be less than zero"* — and the
  carryover is *"the excess of your NOL deduction over your modified taxable income"* (i172). The 2024 Schedule 1 line 8a
  **listed** (15,778); that is the instructions' loss-year convention and Form 172 line 23 neutralises it (1040 SOP **M7**:
  *nothing is burned*). ⛔ **So "Amount Used in Prior Years" for the 2023 loss is 0, not 15,778.**
- 🔎 **Is 2024's Form 172 line 23 (15,778) an error? — Lilian's question, 2026-10-09, after the Melnyk case.** *(Checked by
  two irs.gov researchers and an adversarial judge.)*
  - **Line 23 is not a separate error.** It must take back out exactly what Schedule 1 line 8a put into AGI: line 1 starts
    from AGI, and line 23 is combined into line 24. *(That is derived from the form's arithmetic; no IRS sentence states it.)*
    If they differed, the 2024 NOL would be wrong: 8a = 15,778 with line 23 = 0 gives −16,133; 8a = 0 with line 23 = 15,778
    gives no NOL at all.
  - **What is technically overstated is Schedule 1 line 8a.** Under the limit as the IRS states it — the deduction *"cannot
    exceed"* the pre-2018 NOLs plus the lesser of the post-2017 NOLs and 80% of taxable income figured without the NOL, QBI
    and §250 deductions (i172, *Reminders*) — the 2024 deduction **allowed** was **0**: taxable income before the NOL was
    −29,555, and 80% of nothing is nothing. *(This assumes the whole 15,778 arose after 2017 — D19.)* So a return that
    applied the limit would show **0 on 8a and 0 on line 23**.
  - **Effect: none, on what was checked.** Taxable income is 0 either way. No EIC was claimed. Form 8962 line 3 floors at
    zero (*"If the total is less than zero, enter -0-"*), so it reads 0 either way. Line 24 is −355 either way, and the
    carryover is 16,133 either way. Florida has no individual income tax, so there is no state return to move. **Not
    amending is a judgment, not an IRS rule.**
  - **It has the same root as Melnyk's 2025 return, but sits on a different line.** On Melnyk, an income year, 8a held
    the capped deduction and only line 23 showed the whole carryforward. That inconsistency changed no figure, because there
    is no NOL in an income year. Here the whole amount went on **8a** itself, so line 23 **agrees** with 8a, and both are
    above the 0 the limit allows.
  - **Her doubt about the word is justified.** The IRS uses *"NOL deduction"* in both senses:
    - the **whole amount carried in** — *"your NOL deduction is the total of these carrybacks and carryovers"*, and the
      carryover formula;
    - the **capped amount** — the *"cannot exceed"* limitation.

    The word does not settle it. What settles it is the form's arithmetic, the 80% limit, and Pub 536's own rewrite of the
    sentence the full-listing convention rests on: *"your NOL deduction is the total NOL"* (2017) became *"cannot exceed
    the sum of"* (2022).
  - ⚠️ **This contradicts the firm's 1040 SOP M7**, which tells preparers to list the whole carryforward in a loss year. It
    is flagged there as an open question for Lilian (FOLLOW-UPS row 204). **The 2025 return does not depend on it:** 2025

### 3H · Sales tax — why it is not on this return *(Lilian asked, 2026-10-08)*

- **It is a state tax, not federal.** The 2025 Schedule C instructions (line 23, read on irs.gov) treat it two ways only:
  tax **collected from customers and paid over** to the state is *"not included in gross receipts… nor… a deductible
  expense"* (a vendor allowance the state lets the seller keep goes on line 6); tax **paid on the business's own
  purchases** is *"part of the cost of the property"* — inside supplies or cost of goods, never on line 23. So the
  return never "splits" sales tax: it is out of income and out of expenses.
- **His goods.** Florida has made **marketplace providers** (Amazon) collect and remit the tax on the sales they
  facilitate since **1 July 2021**; the seller does not collect it and leaves those sales off any Florida return.
  ⚠️ *Read in search excerpts of Florida DOR's TIP 21A01-03 and §212.05965 — the Florida sites refused this session's
  connection; not read on the primary page.* A seller must register and collect **only on sales outside the
  marketplace** — his own Shopify store, direct sales — at 6% plus the county surtax (**Broward 1%**, per the 2025
  state surtax table, also from excerpts). **Whether he sells outside Amazon is unknown** — §6 item 8, optional.
- **His receipts figure (50,546)** is money he received, so tax Amazon collected is not in it. **If a 2025 Form 1099-K
  exists, its gross may or may not include the tax Amazon collected** — sources disagree and it has varied by year;
  the 1099-K's own information page says. None is in the firm's files.
- **Services:** personal training and consulting/training are not among the services Florida taxes (excerpts of DOR
  rulings TAA 14A-008, 19A-015) — nothing to do for either spouse's services. ⚠️ A course sold as **pre-recorded video**
  has been held subject to Florida's communications services tax (TAA 23A-009) — raised only, not a 2025 return item.
