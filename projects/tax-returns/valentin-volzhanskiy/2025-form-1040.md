## 0 · The year in one paragraph

Valentin is a **bartender** who works for several unrelated hospitality employers and, separately,
takes **contractor work paid on Form 1099-NEC**. He files **Single**, has **no dependants**, lives in
**Florida** (so no state return has ever been required), and buys his health insurance on the
**Marketplace**, which puts **Form 8962** on his return every year. 2025 differs from 2024 in three
ways that matter, and **each one moves money**: his **contractor income went up by an order of
magnitude** while his W-2 wages went down; **2025 is the first year of the tips deduction**, and he
is a tipped worker in a listed occupation; and his Schedule C, which ran at a **loss** in 2024 —
producing a **qualified-business-loss carryforward into 2025** — will run at a **profit** in 2025,
which turns on **self-employment tax** and, for the first time, makes the **self-employed health
insurance deduction** available, and that deduction is **circular with the premium tax credit**.

🛑 **The return was not prepared.** Four things block it (§6). The most important is not a missing

> 🔑 **THE MULTI-EMPLOYER WORKSHEET IS THE WHOLE ANSWER, AND IT IS NOT ON THE FORM.**
> There is **no standalone Schedule 1-A instruction booklet** (`i1040s1a.pdf` returns 404). The
> instructions live inside the **2025 Instructions for Form 1040**, and the
> **"Qualified Tips From More Than One Employer Worksheet — Keep for Your Records"** is on
> **page 104**. Verbatim structure: for **each employer**, column (b) = qualified tips reported by
> that employer on Form W-2 **or** reported by the employee to that employer on Forms 4070;
> column (c) = qualified tips on Form 4137 column 1(c) for that same employer; column (d) = **the
> greater of (b) or (c)**. Then *"Add lines 1A through 1E, column (d), and enter this amount on
> Schedule 1-A, line 4c."*
> 🔑 **The comparison is made per employer, not on the combined totals.** That is the point of it.
> ⚠️ **Unsettled in the IRS text, not by us:** the worksheet has exactly five rows and, unlike the
> line-5 worksheet, carries **no** *"complete as many copies as needed"* instruction. He has four
> employers, so it does not bite here.
> ⚠️ **Also unsettled:** the **form** says enter `-0-` on lines 4a **and** 4b; the **instructions'**
> Line 4b paragraph only redirects to the worksheet without repeating `-0-`. Both are reported;
> neither is harmonised by us.

> 🟢 **BOX 14 COUNTS — THIS IS THE FINDING THAT PAYS FOR THE WHOLE REVIEW.**
> **Notice 2025-69 §III.A.1**, the Treasury/IRS administrative determination for tax year 2025,
> option **3**, verbatim: *"If an employer voluntarily chooses to report the amount of an employee's
> cash tips in **box 14 of Form W-2** (or on a separate statement), the employee **may use this
> amount** in determining the amount of qualified tips for tax year 2025."*
> Restated at **2025 Instructions for Form 1040, page 103**, method 3.
> 🔑 **The four methods are ALTERNATIVES, not a hierarchy** — *"you can figure your qualified tips
> using one of the methods described in paragraphs 1 through 4"* — and **option 3 is NOT conditioned
> on box 7 being empty.**
> ⚠️ **Its condition is that the EMPLOYER chose to report it.** The employee may not self-compute a
> box-14 figure. Employer 1's W-2 carries `TIPS 16,647.75` in box 14, which satisfies that.
> ⛔ **Authority correction, caught in verification:** this is **not** OBBBA §70201(k). That
> subsection reaches only §§6041(a), 6041(d)(3), 6041A(a), 6041A(e)(3), 6050W(a) and 6050W(f)(2) —
> the **1099/payor** side — and **does not list §6051(a)(18)**, the Form W-2 provision. The
> permission is the administrative determination in Notice 2025-69 §III.A.1.

> ✅ **BARTENDER IS A LISTED OCCUPATION** — category *"Beverage and food service"*, Treasury Tipped
> Occupation Code **101**, *"Bartenders"*. His 2024 return states his occupation as **Bartender** on

> ⚠️ **The occupation filter runs first:** only tips received *in* a listed occupation are qualified.
