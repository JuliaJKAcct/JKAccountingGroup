> 🟢 **BOX 14 COUNTS — THIS IS THE FINDING THAT PAYS FOR THE WHOLE REVIEW.**
> **Notice 2025-69 §III.A.1**, the Treasury/IRS administrative determination for tax year 2025,
> option **3**, verbatim: *"If an employer voluntarily chooses to report the amount of an employee's
> cash tips in **box 14 of Form W-2** (or on a separate statement), the employee **may use this
> amount** in determining the amount of qualified tips for tax year 2025."*
> Restated at **2025 Instructions for Form 1040, page 103**, method 3.
> 🔑 **The four methods are ALTERNATIVES, not a hierarchy** — *"you can figure your qualified tips
> using one of the methods described in paragraphs 1 through 4"* — and **option 3 is NOT conditioned
> on box 7 being empty.**
> ⚠️ **Its condition is that the EMPLOYER chose to report it.** The employee may not self-compute a
> box-14 figure. Employer 1's W-2 carries `TIPS 16,647.75` in box 14, which satisfies **that** condition.
>
> ### 🔴 BUT THE REPORTING ROUTE IS NOT ELIGIBILITY — AND TWO SCREENS BITE BEFORE IT
>
> 🛑 **Notice 2025-69 settles WHERE the figure may be read from. It does not make the amount a
> qualified tip.** ⛔ **An earlier pass of this paper treated "the employer reported it in box 14" as
> the whole test. It is not.** Two statutory screens run first, and on **these** employers the first
> one is live:
>
> **① PAID VOLUNTARILY — service charges and automatic gratuities are OUT.** Verbatim, 2025
> Instructions for Form 1040 **p. 102**, heading *"Paid voluntarily, not subject to negotiation, and
> determined by the customer/payor"*:
> *"Amounts are qualified tips only if they are paid voluntarily and without any consequence in the
> event of nonpayment. **Qualified tips do not include service charges, automatic gratuities, or any
> other mandatory amounts automatically added to a customer's bill** by the vendor or the
> establishment, unless the customer is expressly provided an option to disregard or modify such
> charges, gratuities, and amounts without consequence."*
> 🔑 **The IRS's own Example 1 is this exact fact pattern:** a restaurant's automatic 18% charge on a
> party of six, printed on the tip line — *"Even though the restaurant distributed the amount to you
> and bussers"*, it is **not** a qualified tip. **Example 2 is the contrast**: a POS device offering

### 3E · 🔴 THE VEHICLE — what 2024 did, why it is wrong, and what it costs in 2025

> 🔑 **Raised by Lilian, 2026-09-12:** *"en 2024 se subieron los gastos del teléfono y la gasolina…
> sin embargo, no se llenó la línea 9… ¿es esto un procedimiento correcto?"* **She is right, and the
> answer has three parts that are different in kind.** Everything below is quoted from the
> **2024** documents — the year in question — and re-checked against 2025.

#### ① Fuel for a business car belongs on LINE 9. Three separate texts say so.

| # | Source | Verbatim |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **2024 Instructions, Line 9** *(page C-7)* | *"If you deduct actual expenses: • **Include on line 9 the business portion of expenses for gasoline**, oil, repairs, insurance, license plates, etc.; and • **Show depreciation on line 13 and rent or lease payments on line 20a**."* 🔑 **Both bullets matter — line 9 takes the OPERATING costs only** |
| 2 | **2024 Instructions, Part V line 48** | *"Include all ordinary and necessary business expenses **not deducted elsewhere on Schedule C**."* |
| 3 | 🔑 **The 2024 FORM's own Part V caption** | *"List below business expenses **not included on lines 8–26, line 27b, or line 30**."* |

🔑 **The third is the strongest, and it is on the face of the form the client signs.** Line 9 sits
inside *"lines 8–26"*, so a car-fuel cost is **excluded from Part V by the form's own words.**
⚠️ *(2025 says the same with the renumbering: "not included on **lines 8-27a**, or line 30".)*

#### ② But the line is the SMALL half. What it changes, and what it does not.

⚖️ **It changes NO dollar and NO tax.** Line 28 is *"Add lines 8 through 27b"*, and both routes land
inside it — same total expenses, same net profit, same AGI, same self-employment tax.
⛔ **So this is a presentation defect, not a dollar defect**, and on its own it would not be worth
amending a closed year for.

🔴 **What it DOES change is that the return stops asking about the vehicle at all**, and that is the
real finding:

> **2024 Instructions, Line 9, *"Information on your vehicle"* — verbatim:**
> *"**If you claim any car and truck expenses, you must provide certain information on the use of
> your vehicle** by completing one of the following. **1.** Complete Schedule C, **Part IV**… **2.**
> Complete **Form 4562, Part V**…"*

🔑 **Read the trigger carefully: it is "if you claim ANY car and truck expenses" — not "if you use
line 9".** Routing the fuel through `Other expenses` does **not** escape the obligation. It only
removes the cue.
⚠️ **And the two texts are worded differently, which is recorded rather than leaned on:** the **instructions** key the duty to *claiming the expense*; the **form's Part IV
caption** keys it to *"claiming car or truck expenses **on line 9**"*. **Which governs a blank line 9 is a reading, not an IRS statement.** ⛔ **But do not read this as cover for 2024:** the captions only diverge *because* of the defect — the instruction requires the expense to be on line 9 in the first place, and the Part IV caption simply assumes it is.

**What went unanswered as a result** — Schedule C Part IV lines 43-47: the **date placed in
service**; the **business / commuting / other mileage** split; whether the vehicle was available for
personal use; whether another vehicle was available; and **"Do you have evidence to support your
deduction?"** with **"If Yes, is the evidence written?"**
🔑 **Those are answered under the Form 1040 jurat**, which covers *"this return and **accompanying
schedules and statements**"*. ⓘ *That the tick is therefore a statement under penalties of perjury
is a sound reading of those two texts — it is not a sentence the IRS prints.*

#### ③ The AMOUNT is the part nobody has tested — and it is the one with money in it

> **Pub. 463, *Actual Car Expenses*:** *"Actual car expenses include: Depreciation · Lease payments ·
> Registration fees · Licenses · Insurance · Repairs · **Gas** · Garage rent · Tires · Oil · Tolls ·
> Parking fees"*
> **And, immediately after — *Business and personal use*:** *"If you use your car for both business
> and personal purposes, **you must divide your expenses between business and personal use. You can
> divide your expense based on the miles driven for each purpose.**"* — with the worked example
> *"You can claim only 60% (12,000 ÷ 20,000) of the cost of operating your car."*

🔴 **The 2024 return deducted the fuel with no percentage anywhere.** Deducting all of it asserts
**100% business use of a car by a bartender**, which is a claim nobody made in writing and nobody
tested. ⚠️ **And 2024's fuel figure is more than DOUBLE that Schedule C's entire gross receipts** —
which is possible in a start-up year and is also exactly the shape that draws an eye.

> **Pub. 463, *How To Prove Expenses*:** *"**Note: You can't deduct amounts that you approximate or
> estimate.**"* · *"You should keep adequate records… You must generally prepare a **written
> record** for it to be considered adequate."*
> **Table 5-1, Transportation row:** *"For car expenses, **the cost of the car and any improvements,
> the date you started using it for business, the mileage for each business use, and the total miles
> for the year**."*

⛔ **The statute itself (§274(d)) could NOT be opened from here** — this environment's network policy
allows `irs.gov` only, and `ecfr.gov`, `uscode.house.gov` and `law.cornell.edu` are all refused. **So
the rule above is stated from Pub. 463, not from the Code**, and it is labelled that way on purpose.

#### ④ 🔴 THE PART THAT COSTS REAL MONEY IN 2025 — and it may already be unfixable

> 🔑 **2025 Instructions, Line 9 — this is the text that governs the 2025 return, verbatim:**
> *"You can take the standard mileage rate for **2025** **only if** you: • **Owned the vehicle and
> used the standard mileage rate for the first year you placed the vehicle in service**, or •
> Leased the vehicle and are using the standard mileage rate for the entire lease period."*
> ⓘ *(The 2024 instruction says the same for 2024 — it governs the 2024 facts, not this
> consequence.)* 🔑 **Read that way the chain is short: the rate is barred in 2025 unless it was
> USED in year one — and Pub. 463's Caution establishes that a return deducting gasoline did not
> use it.**
> **Pub. 463, *Choosing the standard mileage rate*:** *"If you want to use the standard mileage rate
> for a car you own, **you must choose to use it in the first year the car is available for use in
> your business**."* · *"You must make the choice… **by the due date (including extensions) of your
> return. You can't revoke the choice.**"*

🔑 **The two methods are exclusive** — Pub. 463's Caution: using the standard mileage rate you
*"can't deduct… **gasoline (including gasoline taxes)**, oil, insurance, or vehicle registration
fees."*

🛑 **So deducting fuel in 2024 was, in substance, an ACTUAL-EXPENSE claim.** The 2024 Schedule C
ticked box **H — *"started or acquired this business during 2024"*** — so 2024 is very likely the
first year the car was available for use in this business.

> ### ⚖️ BUT THE ANSWER IS NOT THE SAME FOR AN OWNED CAR AND A LEASED ONE, AND FOR AN OWNED CAR
> ### THERE IS A REAL ARGUMENT THE OTHER WAY. Do not state this as settled.
>
> 🔑 **Pub. 463 carries TWO separate rules and they are easy to collapse into one.** They are not
> the same rule and they do not reach the same answer here.
>
> | | The rule | What it does to him |
> |---|---|---|
> | **A** | ***"Choosing the standard mileage rate"*** — *"If you want to use the standard mileage rate for a car you own, you must choose to use it in the **first year the car is available for use in your business**."* | 🔴 **This is the one that bites an OWNED car.** If 2024 was that first year and the return claimed actual fuel, the rate is closed for that car |
> | **B** | ***"Standard mileage rate not allowed"*** — a **closed list**: five or more cars · depreciation by any method **other than straight line** · **MACRS** · **§179** · the **special depreciation allowance** · or *"Claimed actual car expenses after 1997 for a car you **leased**"* | ⚖️ **Read what is NOT on it.** For a car he **OWNS**, *"claimed actual car expenses"* is **not a disqualifier** — every owned-car entry on that list is **depreciation-based**, and **2024's line 13 was blank, so no depreciation of any kind was claimed** |
>
> ✅ **For a LEASED car the two rules agree and the answer is clear:** the standard mileage rate must
> be used *"for the entire lease period"*, **and** claiming actual car expenses on a leased car is

> ⚖️ **For an OWNED car they point different ways** — rule A says the election had to be made in year
> one; list B does not bar him. 🔑 **Rule A is the affirmative requirement and is the better reading**,
> but **the absence of his facts from list B is a genuine argument a reviewer will raise**, and it is
> recorded here so nobody has to rediscover it.
>
> ⛔ **AND THE LOGICALLY PRIOR QUESTION IS NOT SETTLED EITHER: whether the 2024 return made an
> ELECTION AT ALL.** It did not claim a car expense on line 9, gave no vehicle information, and
> identified nothing as a vehicle. 🔑 **A thin argument — but the whole point is that nobody can
> answer it without knowing what the car is.**
>
> 🛑 **So this paper does NOT conclude the rate is closed. It concludes the RISK is real and that it

> ⚠️ **What IS settled either way: an amended 2024 return cannot create the election.** Pub. 463:
> *"You must make the choice to use the standard mileage rate **by the due date (including
> extensions) of your return. You can't revoke the choice.**"*

⚖️ **Why it matters in dollars: 2025's rate is 70 cents a mile** *(2024 was 67)*. For a caterer
driving to events, the mileage method is frequently worth **several times** a fuel-only deduction —
and it needs no receipts beyond a log.
