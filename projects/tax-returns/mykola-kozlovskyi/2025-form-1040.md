### 🔑 Head of household — the analysis, before anyone assumes it

**Three tests, all of which must hold. It is not automatic for either parent, and the assumption
that "both live apart with a child, so both qualify" has a specific hole.**

| # | Test | Where it stands |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Unmarried on 31 December 2025** — or "considered unmarried" under §7703(b), which needs the spouse **out of the household for the last six months** | 🔴 **The divorce date is unknown.** If the divorce was final in 2025, both are unmarried and the test is met. If it was **not**, §7703(b) applies — and the joint organizer answers **"No"** to living apart for the last six months. On that answer, **neither** would qualify |
| 2 | **Paid MORE THAN HALF the cost of keeping up a home for the year** — rent, mortgage interest, property taxes, home insurance, utilities, repairs, and food eaten in the home. **Not** clothing, education, medical, transport, or the value of your own labour | 🔴 **Nothing at all is on file for him.** For her, only **September–December** is documented, and **with no established income, who actually paid the rent is a live question** — money another person gives her to pay it is not paid *by her* |
| 3 | **A qualifying person lived in that home MORE THAN HALF the year** (>182 nights) | 🔴 **The two records contradict each other.** Lilian's understanding is that he lives with at least one daughter almost all year; the organizer says **all three lived with their mother 12 months**. Both cannot be true |

**The hole in "both would qualify":**

- ⚠️ **The same child cannot give both parents head of household.** Each parent needs a **different**
  qualifying child, each living with that parent **more than half the year**. With three children
  that is perfectly possible — but it has to be true, child by child, in nights.
- ⚠️ **Two people cannot each pay more than half the cost of the same home.** For any part of 2025
  they shared a home, only one of them was keeping it up. If the separation is late in the year, test
  2 gets hard for whichever of them was not paying.
- 🔑 **And the split that people miss:** residence-based benefits — **head of household, the earned
  income credit and the dependent-care credit — CANNOT be released** to the other parent. Only the
  **child tax credit and the dependency** can, on a signed **Form 8332**. So the custodial parent can
  keep HOH and the EIC *and still hand the child tax credit to the other parent*. That is the lever
  for the dependants decision, and it does not require giving up HOH.

**What settles all of it is one table: each child, and the number of nights in each parent's home in
2025.** Ask for that, not for a conclusion.

⚠️ **And a caution about what HOH is worth here.** Head of household buys a larger standard deduction
and wider brackets. **He has a large loss and no earned income, so on the current picture there is no
positive income for either to shelter and HOH is worth nothing to him.** It becomes worth having only
if the other side of the household turns out to have income — which is
[the open question on her return](../liliia-hlebova-kozlovska/2025-form-1040.md).
