#### 🛠️ The ATX K-1 warning — analysed, and the answer is "change nothing"

> *"KOLO FLORIDA INC — Per Reg. 1.1367-1(c)(2), current-year noncapital, nondeductible expenses in
> excess of basis will not carry forward to reduce stock basis next year. Go to K1 Input to change
> the carryover default."*

✅ **Nothing is missing from the K-1 input, and the default is correct.** _(Analysed 2026-08-19.)_
⚠️ **The citation is ATX's**: §1.1367-1(c)(2) *defines* noncapital, nondeductible expenses; the
**ordering is §1.1367-1(f)** and the **election is (g)**, and the regulation's own text was not
reachable this session — **what follows is quoted from the IRS instructions.**
The Shareholder's Instructions for Schedule K-1 set the basis order: income up → **distributions**
down → **nondeductible expenses** down → **losses** down. Under that default, nondeductible expenses
**in excess of basis are lost**. The **§1.1367-1(g) election** reverses steps 3 and 4 so the excess
*"is treated as an amount described under (3) for the following tax year"* — it carries.

🔴 **It buys nothing here and would bind for ever.** Basis before the nondeductible expenses is
**268,250** against expenses of **252** — line 10 is **267,998**, positive by a wide margin, so
**there is no excess to rescue**. And the order does not matter when basis never runs out:

| | Default | With the (g) election |
|---|---|---|
| after distributions | 268,250 | 268,250 |
| then… | − 252 → 267,998, − 93,055 | − 93,055 → 175,195, − 252 |
| **ending stock basis** | **174,943** | **174,943** |

⚠️ *"Once made, the election applies to the year for which it is made and **all future tax years for
that S corporation, unless the IRS agrees to revoke** your election."* **2024 did not make it, and
the year before a liquidation is the worst possible moment to start.**

📎 **And the election is made by an attached STATEMENT, not by the checkbox** — *"attach a statement
… that states you agree to the carryover rule of Regulations section 1.1367-1(g) and the name of the
S corporation."* **Form 7203 item E only reports that one is in effect.**
🔴 **If one ever WERE in effect, the form changes too:** *"Leave lines 8a, 8b, and 8c blank… If an
election was made, enter this information on **line 13**"*, carrying any prior-year amounts. **Item E
ticked with the 252 still on line 8a would be a wrong Form 7203.** ✅ **Neither applies. Leave both
alone.**

ⓘ **The other warning on that screen is informational and correct:** *"A net operating loss cannot be
carried back to a previous year unless the loss is related to farming."* Post-2020 NOLs carry
forward only. **Nothing to do.**

ⓘ **And the `Comparison` warnings** — wages, self-employment tax, child tax credit, EIC, withholding

### 🆕 Form 172 — the NOL, and the line that decides what reaches 2026

🔑 **This is the most consequential form on his return**, because **2026 is the company's final year**
and the only asset this return produces is a carryforward. _(Added 2026-08-19 after Lilian worked the
return in ATX; the 13,625 itself has been on this paper since 2026-08-17, but the **combined 2026
opening figure had never been stated** — and that is the number 2026 actually needs.)_

#### The question that was open, and how the form answers it

**Does the 2024 NOL of 13,625 get deducted in 2025, or not?** There is a real tension:

- **§172(a)(2)(B)(ii) caps the deduction** at *"80% of the excess (if any) of taxable income computed
  without regard to deductions for NOLs, or Qualified Business Income (QBI), or section 250
  deductions"*. With a Schedule E loss there is **no excess**, so the allowed deduction computes to
  **-0-**.
- **The Form 172 instructions say to enter it anyway:** *"**Deducting a Carryforward.** If you carry
  forward your NOL to a tax year after the NOL year, **list your NOL deduction as a negative figure
  on Schedule 1 (Form 1040)** for the year to which the NOL is carried."* No income condition is
  attached.

✅ **The tension is resolved by Form 172 line 23, and the resolution is that IT DOES NOT MATTER.**

> **Line 23 — NOL Deduction for Losses from Other Years.** *"**You cannot deduct any NOL carryovers
> or carrybacks from other years.** Enter the total amount of your NOL deduction for losses from
> other years."* — entered as a **positive** number and combined into line 24.

🔑 **Line 23 is an ADD-BACK.** It strips whatever other-year NOL you deducted back out of *this*
year's NOL, so **a prior NOL can never create or enlarge the current year's NOL.** Run it both ways:

| Form 172 Part I | **Deduction ENTERED** *(ATX, and the instructions)* | **Deduction = 0** *(the 80% reading)* |
|---|---|---|
| Schedule 1 line 10 → AGI | **(106,680)** | (93,055) |
| **1** ƒ AGI − standard deduction | **(122,430)** | (108,805) |
| **6** Nonbusiness deductions — 🔑 **the standard deduction IS one** | 15,750 | 15,750 |
| 7 · 8 Nonbusiness income | 0 · 0 | 0 · 0 |
| **9** ƒ 6 − 8 | **15,750** | **15,750** |
| 2–5 · 10–22 capital items | 0 | 0 |
| **23** NOL deduction from other years *(positive)* | **13,625** | **0** |
| **24** ƒ combine 1, 9, 17, 21–23 | 🟢 **(93,055)** | 🟢 **(93,055)** |

✅ **IDENTICAL — and the answer is exactly the K-1 loss**, which is the sanity check: an NOL is the
**business** loss, with the nonbusiness standard deduction added back on line 9 and any other-year
NOL added back on line 23. **Enter it the way ATX wants it. The paperwork is different; the tax is
not, and neither is 2026.**

#### 🛑 THE NOL STATEMENT — an e-file BLOCKER, and this paper never mentioned it

🔴 **Reporting an NOL carryover on Schedule 1 line 8a REQUIRES an attached explanatory statement.
Without it the return does not transmit.** ATX raises it as a red **error**, not a warning:

> *"NOL statement must be completed when an NOL carryover amount is reported on Schedule 1."*

⚠️ **This paper gave the figures line by line and never said the statement existed.** _(Lilian hit it
in the software on 2026-08-19 and had to write the justification herself.)_ **A return is not
delivered when its numbers are right — it is delivered when it transmits.** The statement is now in
this paper, in the tie-outs, and in the SOP.

**Where the requirement comes from — stated honestly.** It is **not** in the Form 1040 or Schedule 1
instructions, which say only *"See the Instructions for Form 172 for details."* And it is **not** the
Form 172 caution *"Attach a statement… showing how you figured the 80% limitation, **if applicable**"*
— that is a different requirement and it is **not applicable here**, because the cap permits no
absorption. **What is verifiable is that the software will not transmit**: ATX reports *"NOL
statement must be completed when an NOL carryover amount is reported on Schedule 1"*, which is good
evidence of an e-file schema rule — **but the specific IRS rule has not been located.** In ATX the
form is **`NOL Carryover Explanation (1040)`**. 🔑 **Quote the software as the software.**

**What it has to answer:** which year each NOL arose in · how much · whether the carryback was waived
or unavailable · what has been absorbed since · what is being deducted now · what is left.

📎 **Paste-ready text for this return:**

> **NOL CARRYOVER EXPLANATION**
>
> The net operating loss deduction reported on Schedule 1 (Form 1040), line 8a, is a carryforward of
> a net operating loss of **$13,625** arising in tax year **2024**. The 2024 loss was reported on a
> joint return; the entire loss is attributable to this taxpayer, whose Schedule C and S-corporation
> Schedule K-1 produced it. Figuring each spouse's net operating loss as if a separate return had
> been filed, the other spouse has no business loss and therefore no net operating loss, so all of
> the joint net operating loss is this taxpayer's (Instructions for Form 172, *Joint return in NOL
> year*, step 1).
>
> The loss arose in a tax year ending after December 31, 2020 and is not a farming loss, so no
> carryback is available; it is carried forward under section 172(b)(1).
>
> No part of the 2024 loss has been absorbed in any intervening year. For tax year 2025, taxable
> income computed without regard to the deductions under sections 172, 199A and 250 is not greater
> than zero, so the 80% limitation of section 172(a)(2)(B)(ii) permits no absorption, and modified
> taxable income for the year is zero. The full **$13,625** therefore remains available and is
> carried to tax year 2026.
>
> The amount listed on Schedule 1, line 8a is added back on Form 172, Part I, line 23, so no part of
> it reduced taxable income for 2025.
>
> Tax year 2025 generates a further net operating loss of **$93,055** (Form 172, Part I, line 24).
> The total net operating loss carried to tax year 2026 is **$106,680**.

⚠️ **The line-23 sentence is not optional.** Without it the statement claims a $13,625 deduction and
then says none of it is allowable — which reads, cold, as a contradiction. **The reconciliation is

### ⓘ Why filing status was the most valuable decision on either return — and why it stopped being one

> ✅ **THIS SECTION IS CURRENT — it sits below the superseded block above, but nothing in it depends
> on the old residence facts.** Read it.

**It was never about the standard deduction. Filing status is the PREMIUM-TAX-CREDIT ELIGIBILITY GATE.**

The Instructions for Form 8962 are explicit: a taxpayer filing **married filing separately** who is
not a victim of domestic abuse or spousal abandonment **is not an applicable taxpayer and cannot take
the PTC at all**. The escape is **Exception 1** — someone who meets the §7703(b)
*married-persons-living-apart* requirements and files as **head of household** may file *as if
unmarried* and take the PTC.

| If the divorce was… | Filing status | Premium tax credit |
|---|---|---|
| ✅ **Final in 2025** *(what happened)* | Single, or HOH if the three tests are met | **Available either way** |
| NOT final at 31 Dec 2025 | MFJ, or **MFS**, or HOH via §7703(b) | 🔴 **MFS = no PTC at all.** The whole advance credit becomes excess and is repaid, subject only to the repayment limitation |

🔑 **The stake, so the next case is recognised early.** This household reconciled an advance credit
of **27,460** in 2025. Had the divorce slipped a few days into January, **the difference between HOH
and MFS would not have been a bracket — it would have been the entire credit**, and the §7703(b)
route depends on a "living apart for the last six months" answer the organizer had already given as
**No**. **Ask for the date of the decree before modelling anything else on a separating household.**

🔴 **What head of household turned out to be worth on this return: nothing — because he does not
have it.** With a large loss and no earned income the bigger standard deduction shelters nothing,
and HOH does **not** create EIC or ACTC eligibility — those need earned income, which only
[his ex-wife](../liliia-hlebova-kozlovska/2025-form-1040.md) has. **Single produces an identical
return** — nothing owed, nothing refunded, exactly as head of household would have. 🔑 **The change
that could have cost him money was losing the DEPENDANTS, not losing the STATUS** — the child tax
credit is what absorbs an excess advance premium tax credit, and he no longer has one. ✅ **On these
figures there is no excess to absorb**, so it costs him nothing at all.
⚠️ **It will not be $0 in 2026**, which is the company's **final** return: a liquidation can produce
real income against which a filing status finally matters. **He will still be Single unless someone
moves in with him** — so the planning question for 2026 is the liquidation, not the household.
