### 🔑 Head of household — the analysis, before anyone assumes it

**Three tests, all of which must hold. It is not automatic for either parent, and the assumption
that "both live apart with a child, so both qualify" has a specific hole.**

| # | Test | Where it stands |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Unmarried on 31 December 2025** — or **"considered unmarried"**, which Pub 501 states as **five tests, not one**; the spouse being **out of the household for the last six months** (§7703(b)) is *necessary, not sufficient* | 🔴 **The divorce date is unknown.** If the divorce was final in 2025, both are unmarried and the test is met. If it was **not**, §7703(b) applies — and the joint organizer answers **"No"** to living apart for the last six months. On that answer, **neither** would qualify |
| 2 | **Paid MORE THAN HALF the cost of keeping up a home for the year** — rent, mortgage interest, real estate taxes, insurance on the home, repairs, utilities and **food eaten in the home**; **not** clothing, education, medical treatment, vacations, life insurance, transport, or the value of your own services _(Pub 501, "Keeping Up a Home")_ | 🔴 **Nothing at all for him** was found in Double, Drive or Julia's Gmail in the searches of 2026-08-18. For her, only **September–December** is documented. ⓘ **Pub 501 Worksheet 1 splits "the amount YOU paid" from "the amount OTHERS paid"** — funds she receives and then spends **do** count as paid by her. What does not: amounts another person pays **directly**, and amounts paid out of funds received **in a child's name** (the child's Social Security, for instance), which count as the child's |
| 3 | **A qualifying person lived in that home MORE THAN HALF the year** (>182 nights) | 🔴 **The two records contradict each other.** Lilian's understanding is that he lives with at least one daughter almost all year; the organizer says **all three lived with their mother 12 months**. Both cannot be true |

**The hole in "both would qualify":**

- ⚠️ **The same child cannot give both parents head of household.** Each parent needs a **different**
  qualifying child, each living with that parent **more than half the year**. With three children
  that is perfectly possible — but it has to be true, child by child, in nights.
- ⚠️ **Two people cannot each pay more than half the cost of the same home.** For any part of 2025
  they shared a home, only one of them was keeping it up. If the separation is late in the year, test
  2 gets hard for whichever of them was not paying.
- 🔑 **And the split that people miss:** residence-based benefits — **head of household, the earned
  income credit and the dependent-care credit — CANNOT be released** to the other parent. Only the
  **child tax credit and the dependency** can, on a signed **Form 8332**. So the custodial parent can
  keep HOH and the EIC *and still hand the child tax credit to the other parent*. That is the lever
  for the dependants decision, and it does not require giving up HOH.

**What settles all of it is one table: each child, and the number of nights in each parent's home in
2025.** Ask for that, not for a conclusion.

### 🔴 Why HOH may be the most valuable decision on either return

**It is not about the standard deduction. Filing status is the PREMIUM-TAX-CREDIT ELIGIBILITY GATE.**

The Instructions for Form 8962 are explicit: a taxpayer filing **married filing separately** who is
not a victim of domestic abuse or spousal abandonment **is not an applicable taxpayer and cannot take
the PTC at all**. The escape is **Exception 1** — someone who meets the §7703(b)
*married-persons-living-apart* requirements and files as **head of household** may file *as if
unmarried* and take the PTC.

| If the divorce was… | Filing status | Premium tax credit |
|---|---|---|
| **Final in 2025** | Single, or HOH if the three tests are met | Available either way |
| **NOT final at 31 Dec 2025** | MFJ, or **MFS**, or **HOH** via §7703(b) | 🔴 **MFS = no PTC at all.** The whole advance credit becomes excess and is repaid, subject only to the repayment limitation. **HOH is the only route to the credit** |

⚠️ **The 2024 return reconciled an advance credit in the tens of thousands.** If that scale repeats
in 2025 and the divorce was not final, **the difference between HOH and MFS is not a bracket — it is
the entire credit.**

ⓘ **What HOH is NOT worth here:** with a large loss and no earned income the bigger standard
deduction shelters nothing, and HOH does **not** create EIC or ACTC eligibility — those need earned
income, which is [the open question on her return](../liliia-hlebova-kozlovska/2025-form-1040.md).
**The value is the PTC gate, and it bites hardest if the marriage had not ended by 31 December.**
