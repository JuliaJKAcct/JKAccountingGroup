### ✅ Head of household — the analysis, now that it is settled

**Three tests, all of which must hold. Kept in full because the reasoning is the reusable part — the
assumption that "both live apart with a child, so both qualify" has a specific hole.**

| # | Test | Where it stands |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Unmarried on 31 December 2025** — or **"considered unmarried"**, which Pub 501 states as **five tests, not one**; the spouse being **out of the household for the last six months** (§7703(b)) is *necessary, not sufficient* | ✅ **MET.** The divorce was **final in late December 2025** _(Lilian, 2026-08-18)_, so both are simply **unmarried** and the §7703(b) route — on which the joint organizer's "No" answer would have sunk them both — is never reached |
| 2 | **Paid MORE THAN HALF the cost of keeping up a home FOR THE YEAR** — rent, mortgage interest, real estate taxes, insurance on the home, repairs, utilities and **food eaten in the home**; **not** clothing, education, medical treatment, vacations, life insurance, transport, or the value of your own services _(Pub 501, "Keeping Up a Home")_ | 🟡 **ANSWERED ON *WHO*, OPEN ON *WHEN*.** **He paid his housing from his savings** _(Lilian, 2026-08-18)_ — ⓘ **Pub 501 Worksheet 1 splits "the amount YOU paid" from "the amount OTHERS paid"**, and the *source* of your own money is irrelevant; savings are yours. What would **not** count: amounts another person pays **directly**, and amounts paid out of funds received **in a child's name**. ⚠️ **But the test is annual and the household separated mid-year** — January–August is unestablished for both of them |
| 3 | **A qualifying person lived in that home MORE THAN HALF the year** (>182 nights) | ✅ **MET.** Svitlana and Anna lived with him **all year** _(Lilian, 2026-08-18)_. ⚠️ **The joint organizer says all three lived with their mother 12 months** — that answer is wrong, like several others on it, and the correction is the client's own |

**The hole in "both would qualify" — worth keeping, because it is what makes this case work:**

- ⚠️ **The same child cannot give both parents head of household.** Each parent needs a **different**
  qualifying child, each living with that parent **more than half the year**. Here that is exactly
  what the facts give: Anastasia hers, Svitlana and Anna his. **With three children it is possible;
  with one it is not.**
- 🟡 **Two people cannot each pay more than half the cost of the same home — RULED, but the evidence
  is thin.** For any part of 2025 they shared a home, **only one of them was keeping it up**, and the
  test is stated over the **whole year**. ✅ **Lilian ruled on 2026-08-19 that they lived apart and
  that he has a different address.** 🔴 **The paperwork looks the other way** — the lease on his
  ex-wife's apartment, 1 Sep 2025 → 31 Aug 2026, **names both of them as tenants**, and her share of
  the 3,400 rent is 2,200. ⚠️ **A lease in two names is not evidence of living together**, and this
  is the case that proves it — but **January–August is still undocumented on both sides**, so collect
  it rather than relying on the ruling alone. ⚠️ **Do not dispose of the test by saying each of them
  kept up their own home** as a matter of law — Pub 501 does not say that; here it is true as a
  **fact**, on Lilian's ruling.
- 🔑 **And the split that people miss:** residence-based benefits — **head of household, the earned
  income credit and the dependent-care credit — CANNOT be released** to the other parent. Only the
  **child tax credit and the dependency** can, on a signed **Form 8332**. So the custodial parent
  keeps HOH and the EIC *and still hands the child tax credit to the other parent*. **That is the
  lever the whole household decision turns on, and it costs the signer nothing.**

### ⓘ Why filing status was the most valuable decision on either return — and why it stopped being one

**It was never about the standard deduction. Filing status is the PREMIUM-TAX-CREDIT ELIGIBILITY GATE.**

The Instructions for Form 8962 are explicit: a taxpayer filing **married filing separately** who is
not a victim of domestic abuse or spousal abandonment **is not an applicable taxpayer and cannot take
the PTC at all**. The escape is **Exception 1** — someone who meets the §7703(b)
*married-persons-living-apart* requirements and files as **head of household** may file *as if
unmarried* and take the PTC.

| If the divorce was… | Filing status | Premium tax credit |
|---|---|---|
| ✅ **Final in 2025** *(what happened)* | Single, or HOH if the three tests are met | **Available either way** |
| NOT final at 31 Dec 2025 | MFJ, or **MFS**, or HOH via §7703(b) | 🔴 **MFS = no PTC at all.** The whole advance credit becomes excess and is repaid, subject only to the repayment limitation |

🔑 **The stake, so the next case is recognised early.** This household reconciled an advance credit
of **27,460** in 2025. Had the divorce slipped a few days into January, **the difference between HOH
and MFS would not have been a bracket — it would have been the entire credit**, and the §7703(b)
route depends on a "living apart for the last six months" answer the organizer had already given as
**No**. **Ask for the date of the decree before modelling anything else on a separating household.**

ⓘ **What head of household is NOT worth on this return:** with a large loss and no earned income the
bigger standard deduction shelters nothing, and HOH does **not** create EIC or ACTC eligibility —
those need earned income, which only [his ex-wife](../liliia-hlebova-kozlovska/2025-form-1040.md)
has. **His HOH is worth $0 in 2025** — Single produces the identical return — **which is why the one
unsettled test does not block anything.** ⚠️ **It will not be worth $0 in 2026**, which is the
company's **final** return: a liquidation can produce real income against which a filing status
finally matters, and the cost-of-keeping-up-a-home evidence should be collected as a habit now.
