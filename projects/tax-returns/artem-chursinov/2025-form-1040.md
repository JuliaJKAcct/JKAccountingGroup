## 3 · Form 8858 — is there a foreign branch? (the decision)

> **Asked by Lilian, 2026-10-15:** *"necesito que me digas tu criterio: si tenemos que someter esta forma o no."*
> **Sources:** Form 8858, Schedule M and their instructions (Rev. 12-2024, the current revision — irs.gov "About Form 8858" shows no later development), Pub. 514 and Pub. 54 (2025), IRM 20.1.9 and 8.11.5, Rev. ### 3.1 The answer

🟢 **Yes — file Form 8858 for 2025.** On the facts the documents establish, the couple **operated a foreign branch in Russia in 2025**, at the very least from **16 July 2025**, when both owners moved their own work there, and arguably all year. **Confidence: about 65%** — a judgement on facts and circumstances, not a bright line. **The decision is Julia's** (she signs); an earlier session's reading, recorded in the client file on 2026-10-15, was "no branch" and then "borderline" — §3.7 says where this analysis departs from it and why.

🔑 **And file it as a branch that is NOT a qualified business unit for 2025** — U.S.-dollar functional currency, Schedule C-1 empty, no section 987 — because no books were kept during the year (§3.4). That is the position the facts support, and it is also the only one that can be filed correctly today.

### 3.2 The definitions that decide it

- **Form 8858 instructions, "Foreign Branch (FB)":** *"An FB is defined in Regulations section 1.367(a)-6T(g). For purposes of filing a Form 8858, an FB also includes a qualified business unit (QBU) (as defined in Regulations section 1.989(a)-1(b)(2)(ii)) that is foreign."* → **two alternative tests; meeting either one requires the form.** Schedule G line 6 (*"Is the FDE or FB a qualified business unit…?"*) confirms that a branch need not be a QBU.
- **Test A — Reg. §1.367(a)-6T(g)(1), as the IRS itself restates it (Rev. Proc. 2020-30 §2.04, verbatim):** *"…activities that, based on all the facts and circumstances, constitute an integral business operation carried on by a U.S. person outside the United States. For this purpose, evidence of the existence of a foreign branch includes, but is not limited to, the existence of a separate set of books and records and the existence of an office or other fixed place of business used by employees or officers of the U.S. person in carrying out business activities outside the United States. … activities outside the United States are deemed to constitute a foreign branch if the activities constitute a permanent establishment under the terms of a treaty…"*
- **Test B — a foreign QBU, Reg. §1.989(a)-1(b)(2)(ii):** activities of an individual qualify *"if— (A) The activities constitute a trade or business; and (B) A separate set of books and records is maintained with respect to the activities"* (text from a search excerpt of the regulation, corroborated by Rev. Proc. 2020-30 §2.03 and IRS field advice FSA 200217002). **"An individual is not a QBU"** — but an individual's *activities* can be one (T.D. 9794, verified).
- **Instructions, "Accounting books and records":** *"The existence of a separate set of books and records, or lack thereof, may **affect** the determination…"* — evidence, not a gate.
- **The treaty route is closed for 2025:** the US suspended Articles 5–21 and 23 of the US–Russia treaty from **16 Aug 2024** (IRS Russia-treaty page). **Article 5 (permanent establishment) is suspended — so there is no automatic "deemed branch", and no "no PE under the treaty" defence either.** The facts-and-circumstances test decides.

### 3.4 Test B — a foreign QBU? Not for 2025

- **(A) A trade or business:** met.
- **(B) "A separate set of books and records is maintained":** **not met for 2025** on the client's own written answer. ⓘ *Russian law did require one: an ИП on the patent system must keep an income book (Tax Code Art. 346.53, per Russian secondary sources — the statute itself could not be reached). They did not keep it, contrary to what the client believes — a Russian compliance gap, and one more confirmation that no books existed during the year.* The client's answer — *"we do not keep them … the book I sent you was made by hand in the last few days"* (A8). The bank statements of the dedicated ИП account are the best argument the other way (the regulation accepts "similar records", and a cash-method journal is a list of receipts and payments); the IRS field advice looks at whether records existed during the year. **A ledger built after year-end from bank data is the weakest case, and the client's own answer forecloses the stronger one.**
- 🔴 **Why it matters so much — section 987 now reaches individuals.** The final section 987 regulations (T.D. 10016) apply to **individuals** for taxable years beginning after 31 Dec 2024 — **2025 is the first year** (§1.987-1(b)(1)(i), §1.987-15(a)(1); Notice 2026-17: *"applicable to individuals, domestic corporations, and foreign corporations that are CFCs"*). **A QBU keeping ruble books has the ruble as its functional currency and becomes a "section 987 QBU"**: Schedule C-1, a **Form 8964-TRA** transition statement, the first-year elections on **Form 8964-ELE** (which are free only on a return filed on time — **today**), and a §987 gain or loss computed from balance sheets at 31 Dec 2024 and 31 Dec 2025 that we do not have.
- **Pub. 514 (2025):** *"The U.S. dollar is the functional currency for all taxpayers except some qualified business units (QBUs). A QBU is a separate and clearly identified unit of a trade or business that maintains separate books and records."* ⇒ **No QBU → the functional currency is the dollar**, ruble items are translated into dollars, there is no section 987 and no Schedule C-1.
- ⚠️ **The risk in this position, said plainly.** Practitioners lean towards treating almost any record-keeping as "books" — the regulation accepts "similar records", and the ИП account statements are a list of every receipt and payment. **If the IRS later says the branch WAS a ruble QBU in 2025, the first-year section 987 elections — above all the small-business election, which sets the pre-transition gain or loss to zero — will have been lost, because they exist only on a return filed on time.** A protective route exists: treat it as a ruble QBU today, with **Form 8964-TRA Part I** making the small-business election (Parts II–III are then not completed) and **Form 8964-ELE**, at the price of a 2025 §987 computation from balance sheets the firm does not have. **That trade-off is part of Julia's D3.**
- ⚠️ **2026 is different.** The client says they are reorganising — from now on everything goes through her ИП, with contractors and a 6% simplified-system part. **If they keep real ruble books from 2026, the branch becomes a QBU with ruble functional currency and section 987 starts** — with its first-year elections due on the **timely** 2026 return. **Flag for next year (§7).**

### 3.6 How many forms — one business, two spouses

### 9.5 The "Initial Form 8858" box (D7) — analysed 2026-10-15 late

**What the box means.** The instructions (Rev. 12-2024): *"Check the Initial Form 8858 box in the year the tax owner formed or aquired the FDE or FB."* The 2019–2021 revisions also tied it to an event ("purchase", "formation or purchase"). **It never means "first filing", and no box on the form says "filed before".** So it cannot be set to manage appearances either way: it states when the branch began.

**When did the branch begin?** A branch is *"an integral business operation carried on by a U.S. person outside the United States"* (§3.2). Both parts must be true at once:
- **Late Dec 2024 – 15 Jul 2025:** they were U.S. persons, but by the client's own answer they ran every core function from Florida (A2, A3). The return already treats that period as a U.S. business (QBI, D21) and its income as U.S.-source (§8). §3.1 and §3.3 call it only "arguable".
- **From 16 Jul 2025:** both owners ran it from Russia — a branch on any reading.

The judge rejected it because a branch cannot exist before its operator is a U.S. person carrying it on abroad, and the registrations of 2012/2016 are not entities.
