## 3 · Form 8858 — is there a foreign branch? (the decision)

### 3.1 The answer

🟢 **Yes — file Form 8858 for 2025.** On the facts the documents establish, the couple **operated a foreign branch in Russia in 2025**, at the very least from **16 July 2025**, when both owners moved their own work there, and arguably all year. **Confidence: about 70%** — a judgement on facts and circumstances, not a bright line. **The decision is Julia's** (she signs); an earlier session's reading, recorded in the client file on 2026-10-15, was "no branch" and then "borderline" — §3.7 says where this analysis departs from it and why.

🔑 **And file it as a branch that is NOT a qualified business unit for 2025** — U.S.-dollar functional currency, Schedule C-1 empty, no section 987 — because no books were kept during the year (§3.4). That is the position the facts support, and it is also the only one that can be filed correctly today.

### 3.2 The definitions that decide it

- **Form 8858 instructions, "Foreign Branch (FB)":** *"An FB is defined in Regulations section 1.367(a)-6T(g). For purposes of filing a Form 8858, an FB also includes a qualified business unit (QBU) (as defined in Regulations section 1.989(a)-1(b)(2)(ii)) that is foreign."* → **two alternative tests; meeting either one requires the form.** Schedule G line 6 (*"Is the FDE or FB a qualified business unit…?"*) confirms that a branch need not be a QBU.
- **Test A — Reg. §1.367(a)-6T(g)(1), as the IRS itself restates it (Rev. Proc. 2020-30 §2.04, verbatim):** *"…activities that, based on all the facts and circumstances, constitute an integral business operation carried on by a U.S. person outside the United States. For this purpose, evidence of the existence of a foreign branch includes, but is not limited to, the existence of a separate set of books and records and the existence of an office or other fixed place of business used by employees or officers of the U.S. person in carrying out business activities outside the United States. … activities outside the United States are deemed to constitute a foreign branch if the activities constitute a permanent establishment under the terms of a treaty…"*
- **Test B — a foreign QBU, Reg. §1.989(a)-1(b)(2)(ii):** activities of an individual qualify *"if— (A) The activities constitute a trade or business; and (B) A separate set of books and records is maintained with respect to the activities"* (text from a search excerpt of the regulation, corroborated by Rev. Proc. 2020-30 §2.03 and IRS field advice FSA 200217002). **"An individual is not a QBU"** — but an individual's *activities* can be one (T.D. 9794, verified).
- **Instructions, "Accounting books and records":** *"The existence of a separate set of books and records, or lack thereof, may **affect** the determination…"* — evidence, not a gate.
- **The treaty route is closed for 2025:** the US suspended Articles 5–21 and 23 of the US–Russia treaty from **16 Aug 2024** (IRS Russia-treaty page). **Article 5 (permanent establishment) is suspended — so there is no automatic "deemed branch", and no "no PE under the treaty" defence either.** The facts-and-circumstances test decides.

### 3.4 Test B — a foreign QBU? Not for 2025

- **(A) A trade or business:** met.
- **(B) "A separate set of books and records is maintained":** **not met for 2025** on the client's own written answer — *"we do not keep them … the book I sent you was made by hand in the last few days"* (Q8). The bank statements of the dedicated ИП account are the best argument the other way (the regulation accepts "similar records", and a cash-method journal is a list of receipts and payments); the IRS field advice looks at whether records existed during the year. **A ledger built after year-end from bank data is the weakest case, and the client's own answer forecloses the stronger one.**
- 🔴 **Why it matters so much — section 987 now reaches individuals.** The final section 987 regulations (T.D. 10016) apply to **individuals** for taxable years beginning after 31 Dec 2024 — **2025 is the first year** (§1.987-1(b)(1)(i), §1.987-15(a)(1); Notice 2026-17: *"applicable to individuals, domestic corporations, and foreign corporations that are CFCs"*). **A QBU keeping ruble books has the ruble as its functional currency and becomes a "section 987 QBU"**: Schedule C-1, a **Form 8964-TRA** transition statement, the first-year elections on **Form 8964-ELE** (which are free only on a return filed on time — **today**), and a §987 gain or loss computed from balance sheets at 31 Dec 2024 and 31 Dec 2025 that we do not have.
- **Pub. 514 (2025):** *"The U.S. dollar is the functional currency for all taxpayers except some qualified business units (QBUs). A QBU is a separate and clearly identified unit of a trade or business that maintains separate books and records."* ⇒ **No QBU → the functional currency is the dollar**, ruble items are translated into dollars, there is no section 987 and no Schedule C-1.
- ⚠️ **2026 is different.** The client says they are reorganising — from now on everything goes through her ИП, with contractors and a 6% simplified-system part. **If they keep real ruble books from 2026, the branch becomes a QBU with ruble functional currency and section 987 starts** — with its first-year elections due on the **timely** 2026 return. **Flag for next year (§8).**

### 3.6 How many forms — one business, two spouses

- **One branch.** The client: *"it is one common business"* (Q3); the regulation: *"the nominal relationship among those activities shall not be controlling"* (search excerpt of §1.367(a)-6T(g)). Two ИП registrations do not make two branches.
- **Two filers.** Under a **qualified joint venture** (IRC §761(f) — spouses filing jointly, both materially participating, no state-law entity; a Russian ИП is not an entity), each spouse takes half the items *"as if they were attributable to a trade or business conducted by such spouse as a sole proprietor"*. Each spouse therefore **operates** (their half of) the branch and is a **Category 1 filer.**
- ✅ **Recommended: two Forms 8858, one per spouse, each reporting 50%**, the **same branch name and the same reference ID** on both, each with a short statement naming the other. It mirrors the two Schedules C exactly, which is what any matching will compare. The instructions are silent on spouses; **one combined form in both names is the alternative**, and less consistent with two Schedules C.
- ⛔ **If the return instead keeps one Schedule C per ИП** (her profit, his loss — the P&Ls as delivered), the 8858 must follow it: one form per spouse showing **that ИП's own figures**. **But that split contradicts the client's description of one business** — and if the couple is *not* a QJV and *not* two businesses, the default is a **partnership**, which here would be an unregistered partnership operating in Russia — a far worse filing (Form 1065 / 8865 territory). **The QJV is the shape that fits the facts.
