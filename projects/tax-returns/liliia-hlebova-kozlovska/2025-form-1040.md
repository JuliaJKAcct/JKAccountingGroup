### 🔑 What each child is worth on this return — the scenario table

**Because this is the question the household actually has to decide**, and because the arithmetic is
counter-intuitive: *where a child sleeps* and *who claims a child* are two different levers worth
very different money.

| Scenario | EIC (27a) | ACTC (28) | SE tax + excess APTC | **Refund** |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| ✅ **The facts: 1 child resides with her, she claims 1** | 4,328 | 1,700 | (2,727) | **3,301** |
| ✅ **The facts + ONE Form 8332 from him** | 4,328 | **2,314** | (2,727) | **3,915** |
| The facts + **two** Forms 8332 | 4,328 | 2,314 | (2,727) | **3,915** ⟵ *no gain* |
| *(hypothetical)* 2 children resided with her | **7,152** | 2,314 | (2,727) | **6,739** |
| *(hypothetical)* all 3 resided with her | **8,046** | 2,314 | (2,727) | **7,633** |

**Three rules explain the whole table**, and they are written up as a reusable module in
[the 1040 SOP](../../sops/form-1040-preparation.md):

1. 🔑 **The EIC follows RESIDENCE and cannot be released.** Moving a child's residence is worth
   **2,824**; moving only the *claim* on a Form 8332 is worth **614**. They are not the same lever.
2. 🔑 **Credits that pay cash need EARNED income.** Her ex-husband has none, so no number of children
   produces a dollar on his return — which is the whole reason the 8332 conversation exists.
3. 🔑 **The ACTC has TWO ceilings** — 1,700 per child **and** 15% × (earned income − 2,500) = **2,314**
   here. The second binds from the second child on, so child #2 adds 614 and child #3 adds **nothing**.
   **One Form 8332, not three.**

⚠️ **A knife-edge that only exists in the two-child scenarios.** The 2-child EIC reaches its maximum
at earned income **17,880** (Rev. Proc. 2024-40 §.06) and she is at **17,925** — **$45 above it**. In
those scenarios every extra dollar of Schedule C deduction would *cost* her ≈**$0.37** (40¢ of EIC
and 14¢ of ACTC lost against 14¢ of SE tax saved). ✅ **On the actual one-child facts this does not
bite** — the 1-child maximum starts at **12,730** and she is far above it — so an extra deduction is
close to **neutral**, which is why the unsettled telephone (below) no longer threatens the refund.

⚠️ **Still provisional in the figures above:** Schedule C line 25 (her share of the telephone) has
not been settled. ⓘ **But it now moves the refund by roughly nothing** — see the knife-edge note.

🔑 **The actual method is worth roughly 8× the simplified one here** — do not default to simplified.
⛔ **But neither can be computed until the income is known:** §280A(c)(5) caps the home-office
deduction at the business's gross income less its other expenses. **It cannot create or increase a
loss.** ⓘ **The excess is not lost — it carries to the next year** _(Pub 587)_ — **but a carryover
cannot be deducted in any year the simplified method is used.** That is a second, independent reason
not to default to simplified.

⚠️ **The exclusive-use test is not evidenced anywhere, and it is the most common disallowance.**
§280A(c)(1) requires the space to be used **regularly AND exclusively** for the business. A 67 ft²
area carved out of a 673 ft² home shared with children is exactly the fact pattern the IRS examines.
**Establish how the space is used before claiming it**, whichever method is chosen.
