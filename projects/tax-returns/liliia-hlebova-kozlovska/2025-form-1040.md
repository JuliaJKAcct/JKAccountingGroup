### 🔑 What each child is worth on this return — the scenario table

**Because this is the question the household actually has to decide**, and because the arithmetic is
counter-intuitive: *where a child sleeps* and *who claims a child* are two different levers worth
very different money.

*(Total tax is 2,568 in every row: the SE tax alone. The 2 of excess advance premium tax credit is
absorbed by the nonrefundable child tax credit on line 19 in each case.)*

🔴 **Look at what the 2026-08-19 expense work did to the two hypothetical rows.** Before it, her
earned income was 17,925 and the 2-child EIC was the **maximum, 7,152**. The extra **1,119** of
deductions took earned income to **16,885 — 995 BELOW the 17,880 plateau** — and the 2-child credit
fell to about **6,750** — **and the ACTC fell with it**, because with two children the 15% ceiling
binds instead of the per-child cap (2,314 → 2,158). **In that scenario the same deductions would have
COST her roughly 400** (−402 of EIC, −156 of ACTC, against +158 of SE tax saved).
On the **actual** one-child facts they **gained her 158**, because the 1-child plateau starts at
12,730 and she is nowhere near it. **Identical expenses, opposite sign, decided entirely by how many
children reside with her.**

**Three rules explain the whole table**, and they are written up as a reusable module in
[the 1040 SOP](../../sops/form-1040-preparation.md):

1. 🔑 **The EIC follows RESIDENCE and cannot be released.** Moving a second child's residence is
   worth **2,880** — **2,422** of it earned income credit, which cannot be released at all, plus the
   458 the claim would have moved anyway. **Moving only the claim on a Form 8332 is worth 458.**
   They are not the same lever, and only one of them can be chosen.
2. 🔑 **Credits that pay cash need EARNED income.** Her ex-husband has none, so no number of children
   produces a dollar on his return — which is the whole reason the 8332 conversation exists.
3. 🔑 **The ACTC has TWO ceilings** — 1,700 per child **and** 15% × (earned income − 2,500) = **2,158**
   here. The second binds from the second child on, so child #2 adds 458 and child #3 adds **nothing**.
   **One Form 8332, not three.**

🔴 **A knife-edge that only exists in the two-child scenarios — and it was CROSSED on 2026-08-19.**
The 2-child EIC reaches its maximum at earned income **17,880** (Rev. Proc. 2024-40 §.06). She was at
17,925 — **$45 above it** — and the expense work took her to **16,885, below it**. On the phase-in
every extra dollar of Schedule C deduction *costs* ≈**$0.37** — **37¢ of EIC** (0.93 × the 40%
phase-in rate) and **14¢ of ACTC** lost against **14¢** of SE tax saved. ✅ **On the actual one-child facts this does not
bite** — the 1-child maximum starts at **12,730** and she is far above it — so an extra deduction is
close to **neutral**, which is why the unsettled telephone (below) no longer threatens the refund.

⚠️ **Still provisional in the figures above:** Schedule C line 25 (her share of the telephone) has
not been settled. ⓘ **But it now moves the refund by roughly nothing** — see the knife-edge note.

Situation 1 all three carry the same agreed number.

simplified method is used**, which is a second, independent reason not to default to simplified.

##### 🔑 The rent is a SHARE, and the lease is in two names

**The lease says 3,400 a month; the deduction uses 2,200.** _(Lilian, 2026-08-19.)_ The renewal for
the apartment runs **1 Sep 2025 → 31 Aug 2026** and names **both her and her ex-husband** as tenants
— but **they lived apart**, he has a different address, and **2,200 is her share of the rent**. Only
what **she** paid enters her home-office computation.

⚠️ **A LEASE IN TWO NAMES IS NOT EVIDENCE THAT TWO PEOPLE LIVED TOGETHER**, and the figure a client
reports as "rent" may be a **share** rather than the lease amount. Both mattered here: read together
and unchecked, the lease would have said the household shared one home from September — which would
have taken head of household away from one of them — and would have put 3,400 into a deduction that
belongs at 2,200.

##### ✅ The renter's insurance — corrected, and now IN the computation

**Policy HO-4, effective 27 Aug 2025 → 27 Aug 2026, total premium 74 for the year.** It is **hers**
and it is **for this apartment** _(Lilian, 2026-08-19)_, so **4/12 of it — 24.67 — is an indirect
home-office expense** _(Pub 587 lists insurance on the home among the indirect expenses)_.
ⓘ **Why 4/12 and not the calendar-year slice.** Pub 587's premium rule alone would give the part of
the premium covering the 2025 tax year (≈4.1/12). What cuts it to **exactly the four months** is the
separate part-year rule: *"You cannot deduct expenses for the business use of your home incurred
during any part of the year you did not use your home for business purposes."* Two rules, and the
narrower one governs.

🔴 **Two corrections this closes.** The worksheet reported **Insurance 0**, which was wrong — the
firm held the policy all along. And **this file repeatedly said her own tenancy began "12 Sep 2025".
No document the firm has read carries a 12 September**: the policy starts **27 August** and the
lease **1 September**, and those are the two dated documents on file. The date was invented by an earlier reading and is struck.

⚠️ **The exclusive-use test is still not evidenced, and it is the most common disallowance.**
§280A(c)(1) requires the space to be used **regularly AND exclusively** for the business. A 67 ft²
area carved out of a 673 ft² home shared with a child is exactly the fact pattern the IRS examines.
**Establish how the space is used before claiming it**, whichever method is chosen.

⚠️ **What is still missing from the worksheet, and every item would INCREASE the deduction:**
1. **Blank ≠ zero** on HOA, water/sewer/trash, cleaning and repairs. Water, sewer and trash commonly
   sit inside "Utilities" and are simply absent.
2. ✅ **RESOLVED — the four-month worksheet and the twelve-month telephone do NOT disagree.**
   _(Lilian, 2026-08-19.)_ **The business and the home office run on two different clocks:** the
   business can have started in January — hence twelve months of phone — while the home office only
   exists from September, when she had one. Pub 587: *"You cannot deduct expenses for the business use
   of your home incurred during any part of the year you did not use your home for business
   purposes."* ⚠️ **But it opens two questions:** if the business ran January–August, **what did it
   cost in those months** (internet, mileage, anything) — and if she worked from her **previous**
   home, that is a **second Form 8829**, because the form's header says one per home.
3. **Mileage has never been asked for.** The 2025 standard rate is **70 cents per mile** _(IRS Notice
   2025-05, verified 2026-08-19)_. For a consultant this is routinely the second-largest deduction
   after the home office, and **nothing was found in Double, Drive or Julia's Gmail** in the searches of
2026-08-18 and 2026-08-19.

ⓘ **How much more is worth chasing — the answer is bounded, and it depends on the Form 8332.**
With taxable income already zero, an extra dollar of deduction saves only the **14.1%**
self-employment tax — but only while the credits hold still.

- **If she claims ONE child** (no 8332): the ACTC's **per-child cap** binds while earned income stays
  above **13,833**, and the 1-child EIC stays at its maximum above **12,730**. From 16,885 that
  leaves **≈3,284** of further deductions worth ~14¢ each _(≈460 of tax)_, then a neutral band, and
  past **≈4,470** each further dollar **costs ≈31¢ net** (34¢ of EIC lost, less the ACTC and SE tax
  effects).
- 🔴 **If the recommended Form 8332 IS signed, the free zone disappears.** With two children claimed
  the ACTC runs on the **15% ceiling** rather than the per-child cap, so it falls with earned income:
  **+14.13¢ of SE tax saved − 13.94¢ of ACTC lost ≈ 0.2¢ per dollar.** The ≈460 collapses to about
  **$6**. **Deductions stop being worth chasing the moment the second child is claimed.**

**Chase the blanks and the mileage — and chase them BEFORE deciding the 8332, not after.**
