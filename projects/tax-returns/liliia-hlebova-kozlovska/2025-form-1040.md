#### 🔑 Why the earned income credit is 7,594 and not the 8,046 maximum

**She is on the PHASE-IN, not the plateau.** The three-child credit reaches its maximum **8,046** at
earned income **17,880** _(Rev. Proc. 2024-40 §.06)_; her earned income is **16,884 — 996 short of
it** — so the credit is still climbing at the statutory **45%** rate. The EIC Table figures each
$50 bracket at its midpoint: 45% × 16,875 = 7,593.75 → **7,594**, which is exactly what the printed
table shows.

🔴 **This is the one place on the return where an extra deduction now COSTS her money — see the
derivation in §3's home-office notes.** On the phase-in every further dollar of Schedule C deduction
is worth about **−42¢**. ⚠️ **So the open expense items reverse sign.** The mileage nobody has asked
for, the home-office blanks, the January–August internet: while she claimed one child these were
worth chasing at 14¢ on the dollar; **with three children they are worth roughly −42¢.**
ⓘ **They still have to be RIGHT** — a deduction is not optional because it is unhelpful — but
**stop presenting them to her as money on the table.**

🔑 **Earned income for both credits is 16,884 = Schedule C line 31 (18,168) − half the SE tax
(1,284)**, not the 92.35% figure on Schedule SE line 4a. Her AGI is the same 16,884, so the
"greater of earned income or AGI" test in the phase-out never bites.

#### ✅ DO NOT PRO-RATE TWICE — the self-check RAN, and the convention is now SETTLED

**The amounts entered are Sep–Dec only** — rent 2,200 × **4**, electricity for **four** months,
insurance 74 × **4/12** rounded to **25**. The worry was that ATX's worksheet also carries the **date
business use of the home began** (`9/1/2025`) and might prorate a *second* time from it, collapsing
the deduction to about a third.

📌 **IT DOES NOT. Recorded 2026-08-20 off the prepared return, so next year does not re-derive it:**

| ATX field | Read back | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| *Date Home Was First Used for Business* | `9/1/2025` | **entered, and it drives NO proration of the expense figures** |
| Form 8829 line 23 col (b) | **9,105** | = 8,800 + 280 + 25 — the period-only amounts, untouched |
| Form 8829 line 24 | **907** | = 9,105 × 9.96% — **the FULL business-use percentage, not 4/12 of it** |

🔑 **So the rule for this client, and for any ATX home office begun mid-year: enter PERIOD-ONLY
amounts.** ⚠️ **The date field is documentation, not a computation** — but it is *also* what drives
depreciation for an owner, so a homeowner client is not covered by this finding.
