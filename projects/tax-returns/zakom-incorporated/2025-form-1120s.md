## 0 · The year in one paragraph

A single-shareholder Florida S corporation running general freight trucking, no employees on
payroll, no QuickBooks and no bookkeeper — the books arrive once a year as the firm's own
spreadsheet template, filled in by the client. 2025 revenue is up about 23% on 2024 while the
client's own profit line collapses to near nothing, and **the collapse is at least partly an
artifact**: one expense line's own label says it includes loan principal. ⚠️ **How much of the fall it
explains is not established** — depreciation also dropped and a home-office allocation was newly
booked. The year also carries six equipment additions, four disposals
including **a truck destroyed and settled by insurance**, personal cars moving through the
business account, and a home-office allocation computed the way a sole proprietor would.

#### ① The reconciliation — books → return, and it ties to the dollar

**Source of the 2024 book figures below: the client's own `Income Statement (2024)` sheet** in the

#### ①-bis 🔑 THE INDEPENDENT CHECK — and it is the one that earns the conclusion

⚠️ **The reconciliation above proves only that the 7,951 is not inside LINE 20.** It says nothing
about lines 9–12 or 16–19, and ⛔ **reading those as blank is exactly what §1 forbids.** The check
that closes them uses two operands neither reconciliation touches:

```
gross receipts                 1,044,320
 − officer compensation           25,000   (page 1 line 7)
 − interest                       12,110   (page 1 line 13)
 − depreciation                   35,861   (page 1 line 14)
 − other deductions              893,925   (page 1 line 20)
 = 77,424  =  page 1 line 22, exactly           ✅
```

🔑 **Page 1 closes to the dollar with no room for another deduction line.** Had the 7,951 been taken
anywhere on page 1, line 22 would read **69,473**. **That — not a blank cell — is what establishes
that it was not deducted.**

🔑 **And a third, free corroboration, from the M-1:** the client's own 2024 book income is
`1,044,320 − 973,895.08 − 25,000` = **45,424.92**, while Schedule M-1 line 1 as filed is **71,515** —
a gap of **26,090.08**, against the depreciation difference *(54,000 − 35,861 = 18,139)*

rounding** *(the books carry 12,110.08, the return 12,110)*. **Not exact, and close enough to be
evidence rather than coincidence.** ✅ **So 2024's M-1 line 1 was built from a TAX-ADJUSTED book column,
not from the client's books**, and it carries neither reconciling item on its face.
🔑 **That also answers §3A's open flag** about the missing depreciation add-back: the adjustment was
made **before** the M-1, inside the book-income figure, rather than on it.

**And the four unreadable captions — their TOTAL is pinned, their individual values are not:** the twelve readable captions sum to

#### ④ Depreciation — the convention is explicit, and it is an ELECTION with a statement

**Page 20 of the 2024 return is an `Elections` page:**

> *"Pursuant to IRC Section 168(k)(2)(D)(iii), the Taxpayer elects out of first-year special
> depreciation for all **3-Year** depreciable property placed in service during the current tax
> year."* — **and the same election for 5-Year property.**

🔑 **So there was no bonus because the firm ELECTED OUT, deliberately, with an attached statement.**
⚠️ **The election out of bonus is made annually, by property class, on a timely filed return — so if
2025 repeats it the statement must be attached again, and if it is not attached bonus applies by
default.** ⛔ **Stated from knowledge and NOT read off the 2025 instructions or the Code — verify
before relying on it**, and note the quoted cite is the software's pre-TCJA wording. **The 2025 bonus
rate also has to be looked up**, because "bonus applies by default" is a different number this year.
**This is the kind of attachment that blocks e-file when it is missing** *(delivery rule 6)*.
