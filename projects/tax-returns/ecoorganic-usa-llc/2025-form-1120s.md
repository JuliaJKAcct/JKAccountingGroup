##### 📘 E-8f · Lilian's questions on Schedule M-2 — the theory in plain words, and the "not required" hypothesis tested

**Her questions, 2026-09-02:** *"¿Hay alguna posibilidad de que en la declaración del 2024 el AAA esté en
cero porque no había obligación de someter el Schedule M-2 en ese año?… Explícame por qué dices que hay una
incongruencia… no entiendo las fórmulas, la teoría detrás de este Schedule."*

**1 · What the AAA is, without formulas.** An S corporation pays no tax itself; its profit is taxed to the
shareholders through the K-1 every year, whether or not they take the money out. The **AAA** is the
corporation's **running tally of profit already taxed to the shareholders and not yet paid out to them**.
It is not cash and it is not a book account — QuickBooks has no such account — it is a memo balance kept
**on the return**, on Schedule M-2 column (a). Its only job is to answer, when money is paid out: *"has this
already been taxed?"* If yes, the distribution is tax-free up to that balance.

**2 · How the schedule moves, year by year.** Start with last year's ending balance. Add this year's taxable
profit. Subtract this year's loss, the separately stated deductions *(§179, charitable)* and the expenses
the law never lets anyone deduct. Subtract the distributions — **but distributions can never push it below
zero**; the part they cannot absorb simply is not entered. *(One refinement from the instructions, for
completeness: in a year where the decreases exceed the increases, that "net negative adjustment" is taken
last, after the distributions — SOP §10.0b simplifies it, and it makes no difference to this return, where
2025 is a profit year and the 2024 reconstruction carries no distributions.)* What is left is the ending balance, **and that ending balance is next year's starting
balance.** *(In the form's numbering: line 1 → lines 2–5 → line 6 → line 7 → line 8, and line 8 becomes
the following year's line 1.)* The IRS instructions describe it as one account: it is **zero only "on the
first day of the corporation's first tax year as an S corporation"**, and every year *"at the end of the
tax year, adjust the AAA for the items… in the order listed"* *(2025 Instructions for Form 1120-S, Schedule
M-2, column (a))*. **Losses CAN drive it negative** — which is why this company's is negative: 2023 lost
money, 2024 lost more.

**3 · When it matters, and when it does not.** It matters when the corporation has **accumulated earnings
and profits** from years as a C corporation — then the AAA is what separates a tax-free distribution from
a taxable dividend — and in a **post-termination transition period**. **This company has no E&P** (§E-2 ④,
corroborated), so for it the AAA decides **nothing about tax**: whether a 2025 distribution is taxable is
decided by **the shareholder's basis — ISHKHAN's, on Julia's date (§B-0)** — and the AAA is a schedule that has to be filled in correctly and
consistently, nothing more. That is why keeping (89,293) costs nothing (§E-8e).

**4 · Why it is an inconsistency, and not an opinion.** One account cannot have two balances at the same
instant. The 2023 return says the balance at **the end of 2023** was **(14,210)**. The 2024 return says the
balance at **the beginning of 2024** was **0**. Those are the same moment — midnight, 31 December 2023 —
and the two returns disagree by 14,210. **The same 2024 return then carries (14,210) as the opening retained
earnings on its Schedule L**, so the preparer's file knew the deficit existed; it simply did not land on M-2
line 1. The consequence is mechanical: 2024's ending (89,293) is 2024's own movement alone, and the true
ending balance is (103,503).

**5 · Her hypothesis — "the 2024 zero could be because the schedule was not required that year" — tested
against the instructions, and it does not hold, for four separate reasons:**

| | |
|---|---|
| ① **The small-company exemption does not cover M-2.** | Schedule B question 11 *(the form's own words: receipts and year-end assets both under 250,000)* exempts **Schedule L and Schedule M-1 only** — the 2025 instructions say, for each of the two, that it *"isn't required to be completed if the corporation answered 'Yes' to question 11 on Schedule B."* **No such sentence exists for Schedule M-2.** *(Read off `i1120s.pdf` from irs.gov on 2026-09-02; the 2024 edition carried the same rule — stated from memory, the exemption has been stable for years.)* |
| ② **What the instructions DO allow is narrower, and it is not "start at zero".** | For a corporation **without** E&P they say it *"doesn't need to maintain the AAA in order to determine the tax effect of distributions"* — and two sentences later that *"it is recommended that the AAA be maintained by all S corporations."* Not maintaining it would mean leaving the column alone; **it never means resetting a balance that exists.** The only zero the instructions prescribe is the first day of the **first** S year. |
| ③ **2024 was not the first S year, and its M-2 was not left blank.** | The 2023 return is itself a Form 1120-S with a completed Schedule M-2 ending at (14,210) — so the S election predates 2024. And the 2024 M-2 **was filled in**: it carries the year's loss and other reductions and ends at (89,293). A schedule that was completed, with a wrong line 1, is not a schedule that was omitted. |
