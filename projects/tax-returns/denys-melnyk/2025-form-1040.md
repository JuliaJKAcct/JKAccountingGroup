### 2D · 🔴 What is NOT certain — the basis, and the missing Form 7203

⛔ **There is no Form 7203 anywhere in the 44-page return.** The string `7203` appears **zero times**
in 440,198 characters of extracted text.

**It was required.** The Form 7203 instructions list four triggers, any one of which compels it, and
he meets the first: *"claiming a deduction for their share of an aggregate loss from an S
corporation"*. Schedule E page 2 states the same duty on its face — *"if you report a loss … you
must check the box in column (e) on line 28 and attach the required basis computation."*
⚠️ **Whether that box was ticked cannot be read from a text extraction** — checkboxes are graphical.

**Why it matters: §1366(d)(1) caps the deductible loss at the shareholder's stock and debt basis.**
Deducting 630,737 in full, as nonpassive, asserts **630,737 of basis**. Nobody has verified it and
no form supports it.

🔑 **And the two possible treatments are opposite in effect:**

| If basis was sufficient (as filed) | If it was not |
|---|---|
| A **net operating loss of 537,926** | A **§1366(d)(2) suspended loss** |
| Usable against **any** income | Usable **only against future income from that same corporation** |
| Capped at **80%** of taxable income | And only when basis is restored |
| Survives a disposal of the stock | ⛔ **Dies with the shares** if basis is never restored |

⚠️ **The absence of the form is not proof the loss was wrong.** If he genuinely contributed that
capital or lent the company that money personally, the loss is allowable and what is missing is the
**form**, not the **basis**. **Both readings are open.**

⛔ **The bank's debt does not count.** In an S corporation — unlike a partnership — entity-level
borrowing gives the shareholder no basis. **For a trucking company with financed equipment this is
the likeliest place for the figure to fail.**

### 2E · 🟠 A second, smaller gap on the same return

**Schedule 1 line 8p — the §461(l) excess-business-loss adjustment — is blank**, and no Form 461
appears. The 2025 threshold is **$313,000 / $626,000 joint**; 2024's was slightly lower. His
aggregate business loss of **631,073** sits on that boundary, so the limitation should at least have
been computed.

✅ **Probably not material to the total**, and for a sound reason: a disallowed excess business loss
*"is treated as a net operating loss (NOL) carryover for subsequent years."* **It lands in the same
bucket.** Recorded so a reviewer sees it was considered, not as an alarm.

### ⚠️ What decision 1 carries with it, and neither point is optional

- 🔴 **Form 8082 is required, not a nicety.** Its instructions compel it where *"the pass-through
  entity hasn't filed a tax return or given you a Schedule K-1 … by the time you're required to file
  your tax return (including extensions) and there are items you must include on your return."* The
  explanation to enter is literally **"Schedule K-1 not received."**
  ⛔ **Omitting it:** any deficiency from conforming his figures to the corporation's return *"may be
  assessed immediately"*, late-filing and late-payment penalties included, **without the normal
  deficiency procedures** — plus a possible §6662 accuracy penalty.
- 🔴 **Illinois.** The K-1 is what states his Illinois-source share; his **2024** return carried an
  Illinois nonresident return. Filing without it risks **omitting a state return**, and
  ⛔ **amending the federal return later does not file a state return that was never filed.**

5. 🟠 **The 2025 vehicle sale** — whose vehicle, ever depreciated, and how many others.
6. 🟠 **Marketplace coverage or not** — pending with his broker.
