### ⚠️ WHY ≈ 60,750 IS A FLOOR, AND THE ERROR RUNS TOWARD THE TAXPAYER

⛔ **An earlier version said capital stock of 1,000 "leaves very little room" for basis the balance
sheet does not show. That is BACKWARDS, and it is struck.** From the 2024 Instructions for Form
1120-S:

> *"On the first day of the corporation's first tax year as an S corporation, **the balance of the
> AAA is zero**."*

🔑 **So AAA never contains what he PAID for his shares.** If he bought stock — from the corporation
or from a co-founder — **that cost is his basis and is invisible on the corporation's return by
construction.** For a 50% holder of a company with 7,642,187 of gross receipts, that is not a
remote possibility.

**Three further asymmetries, all running the same way:**
- AAA is increased by income *"other than tax-exempt income"* — **stock basis IS increased by
  tax-exempt income.**
- AAA is decreased by deductible losses **and nondeductible expenses**, whether or not any
  shareholder could deduct them — **prior suspended losses depress AAA below basis.**
- *"The AAA may have a negative balance at year end"* — **basis cannot go below zero.**

⚠️ **And on reliability:** *"An S corporation without AE&P doesn't need to maintain the AAA."* **This
corporation has no AE&P** (§2F-ii), so its AAA is kept by recommendation, not requirement.

### 🟠 What can honestly be said

**The corporate return cannot show his stock basis. What it does show is far too small to support a
630,737 deduction — UNLESS he acquired the stock for real money, made contributions the corporation
never recorded, holds a note of the corporation, or paid under a guarantee.** ⓘ *A guarantee alone
gives no basis — but **a payment made under one does**, and this is a trucking company with financed
equipment and a partner said to be in bankruptcy, so that branch is live.*

⛔ **So the direction is strong and the magnitude is not established.** If the reading holds, most of
that loss should have been **suspended under §1366(d)(2)** rather than deducted, and the **537,926
would be overstated** — with the remainder a suspended loss rather than a free NOL.
⛔ **JULIA'S DETERMINATION. This paper supplies the evidence and the arithmetic and stops.**

🔵 **AND IT MAKES §4A's POINT DECISIVE.** A §1366(d)(2) loss is released when the shareholder has
**basis** again — from income, a contribution **or** a loan. **So reporting 2025 income is the
mechanism by which a suspended loss actually gets used.**

   cheap.**
