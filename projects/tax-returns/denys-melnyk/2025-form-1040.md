### 2D · 🔴 What is NOT certain — the basis, and the missing Form 7203

⛔ **No Form 7203 appears in the text extracted from the 44-page return** — the string `7203` occurs
**zero times** in 440,198 characters. ⚠️ **Bounded claim:** 4 of the 44 pages extracted only
partially (§6); none of the four is form-shaped, but the absence rests on the extraction.
⚠️ **And THREE were required, not one** — the basis limitation is per corporation and Form 7203 is
filed per S corporation. **All three K-1s carried losses.**

**It was required.** The Form 7203 instructions list four triggers, any one of which compels it, and
he meets the first: *"claiming a deduction for their share of an aggregate loss from an S
corporation"*. Schedule E page 2 states the same duty on its face — *"if you report a loss … you
must check the box in column (e) on line 28 and attach the required basis computation."*
⚠️ **The column (e) checkbox was not readable in THIS extraction.** *(Not a claim about PDFs in general — a flattened return often renders a tick as text.)*

**Why it matters: §1366(d)(1) caps the deductible loss at the shareholder's stock and debt basis.**
Deducting 630,737 in full, as nonpassive, asserts **630,737 of basis**. Nobody has verified it and
no form supports it.

🔑 **And the two possible treatments are opposite in effect:**

| If basis was sufficient (as filed) | If it was not |
|---|---|
| A **net operating loss of 537,926** | A **§1366(d)(2) suspended loss** |
| Usable against **any** income | Usable **only against future income from that same corporation** |
| Capped at **80%** of taxable income *(computed without the NOL, QBI or §250 deductions)* | And only when basis is restored |
| Survives a disposal of the stock | ⛔ **GENERALLY dies with the shares** if basis is never restored — ⓘ *but see §1366(d)(3), which allows suspended losses in the post-termination transition period to the extent of stock basis, and a §1041 transfer to a spouse, which carries them to the transferee* |

⚠️ **The absence of the form is not proof the loss was wrong.** If he genuinely contributed that
capital or lent the company that money personally, the loss is allowable and what is missing is the
**form**, not the **basis**. **Both readings are open.**

⛔ **The bank's debt does not count.** In an S corporation — unlike a partnership — entity-level
borrowing gives the shareholder no basis. **For a trucking company with financed equipment this is
the likeliest place for the figure to fail.**

## 🔴 THE ONE QUESTION THE ANSWER HANGS ON

**Line 10 removes any income on lines 1-8 that is NOT attributable to a trade or business.** The
whole result turns on the **49,000 Form 4797 gain** being a trade-or-business disposal **of his**.
⛔ **Form 4797 also reports gains on property that is not the taxpayer's trade-or-business asset —
the form's title is not the test, line 10 is.** And §7 of this very paper records that disposal as
**"not examined"**.

| If the 49,000 IS trade-or-business | If it is NOT |
|---|---|
| Line 10 = 0 · line 14 = (581,942) | Line 10 = 49,000 → line 13 = (49,000) · line 14 = **(630,942)** |
| **Line 16 = +28,058 → no EBL** | **Line 16 = (20,942) → an EBL of 20,942 on Sch 1 line 8p** |

⚖️ **AND HERE IS WHY THIS IS NOT ALARMING — the 2025 carryforward is 537,926 EITHER WAY.** A
disallowed excess business loss *"is treated as a net operating loss (NOL) carryover for subsequent
years."* So on the second branch: 2024 AGI rises by 20,942, the Form 172 NOL falls to **516,984**,
and the 20,942 arrives separately as an NOL carryover. **516,984 + 20,942 = 537,926.** ✅ **The
figure this paper exists to establish does not move. What moves is the presentation of the 2024
return.**

ⓘ **A robustness point that runs the same way:** the instructions' **Ordering Rules** apply at-risk,
then passive, then excess business loss. So if §2D's basis question resolves against the taxpayer,
the allowable loss is *smaller* — which makes the no-EBL conclusion **safer**, not shakier.

🟠 **AND SEPARATELY, THE FORM WAS OWED WHATEVER THE ARITHMETIC PRODUCED.** Under **Who Must File**:

> *"File Form 461 if you're a noncorporate taxpayer … and either (i) your net losses from all of
> your trades or businesses are more than $305,000 ($610,000 …), or **(ii) you would report a loss
> of more than $152,500 on any one of Form 461, lines 1 through 8**."*

**Line 5 carries (631,073).** ⛔ **So the form had to be filed even on the branch where it computes
to zero.** **Whether that is worth raising with the client, and whether a 2024 return filed by
another firm should be amended, is Julia's call — not this paper's.**
