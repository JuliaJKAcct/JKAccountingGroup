### 2D · 🔴 What is NOT certain — the basis, and the missing Form 7203

⛔ **No Form 7203 appears in the text extracted from the 44-page return** — the string `7203` occurs
**zero times** in 440,198 characters. ⚠️ **Bounded claim:** 4 of the 44 pages extracted only
partially (§6); none of the four is form-shaped, but the absence rests on the extraction.
⚠️ **And THREE were required, not one** — the basis limitation is per corporation and Form 7203 is
filed per S corporation. **All three K-1s carried losses.**

**It was required.** The Form 7203 instructions list four triggers, any one of which compels it, and
he meets the first: *"claiming a deduction for their share of an aggregate loss from an S
corporation"*. Schedule E page 2 states the same duty on its face — *"if you report a loss … you
must check the box in column (e) on line 28 and attach the required basis computation."*
⚠️ **The column (e) checkbox was not readable in THIS extraction.** *(Not a claim about PDFs in general — a flattened return often renders a tick as text.)*

**Why it matters: §1366(d)(1) caps the deductible loss at the shareholder's stock and debt basis.**
Deducting 630,737 in full, as nonpassive, asserts **630,737 of basis**. Nobody has verified it and
no form supports it.

🔑 **And the two possible treatments are opposite in effect:**

| If basis was sufficient (as filed) | If it was not |
|---|---|
| A **net operating loss of 537,926** | A **§1366(d)(2) suspended loss** |
| Usable against **any** income | Usable **only against future income from that same corporation** |
| Capped at **80%** of taxable income *(computed without the NOL, QBI or §250 deductions)* | And only when basis is restored |
| Survives a disposal of the stock | ⛔ **GENERALLY dies with the shares** if basis is never restored — ⓘ *but see §1366(d)(3), which allows suspended losses in the post-termination transition period to the extent of stock basis, and a §1041 transfer to a spouse, which carries them to the transferee* |

⚠️ **The absence of the form is not proof the loss was wrong.** If he genuinely contributed that
capital or lent the company that money personally, the loss is allowable and what is missing is the
**form**, not the **basis**. **Both readings are open.**

⛔ **The bank's debt does not count.** In an S corporation — unlike a partnership — entity-level
borrowing gives the shareholder no basis. **For a trucking company with financed equipment this is
the likeliest place for the figure to fail.**

### 2E · 🔴 A second gap on the same return — §461(l), and it is sharper than it first looked

**Schedule 1 line 8p — the §461(l) excess-business-loss adjustment — is blank**, and no Form 461
appears in the text extracted from the return _(same 4-page extraction limit as §6)_.

**The governing figures are the 2024 ones**, quoted from the **2024** Instructions for Form 461:

> *"For 2024, the threshold amount is **$305,000 ($610,000 for taxpayers filing a joint return)**."*

🔴 **AND FORM 461 HAD TO BE FILED HERE WHATEVER THE ARITHMETIC PRODUCED.** The 2024 instructions
carry a *"What's New — there are new filing rules for 2024"*, and it is the **second** test that
bites:

> *"File Form 461 if you're a noncorporate taxpayer … and either (i) your net losses from all of
> your trades or businesses are more than $305,000 ($610,000 for taxpayers filing a joint return),
> or **(ii) you would report a loss of more than $152,500 on any one of Form 461, lines 1 through
> 8**."*

**The Schedule E loss of 631,073 lands on one of those lines and is more than four times $152,500.**
⛔ **So the obligation to file did not depend on there being an excess business loss at all.**

⚠️ **AND THE 631,073 IS NOT THE §461(l) FIGURE** — an earlier version of this section used it as
though it were, and that was wrong. The instructions define the computation and exclude employment:

> *"Such excess losses should be determined **without regard to any deductions, gross income, or
> gains attributable to any trade or business of performing services of an employee**."*

**So the W-2 comes OUT, while the Form 4797 ordinary gain and the spouse's Schedule C go IN as
business income and net against the loss first.** On the figures in §2B the excess over the joint
threshold is `631,073 − (4797 gain + spouse's Schedule C) − 610,000`, i.e. **21,073 less those two
items — and it may well be zero.** ⛔ **This paper does not have that split, so it cannot compute it.**

⛔ **NOT RESOLVED HERE, and an earlier version wrongly called it "probably not material".** That is a
determination about a prior preparer's return made without the arithmetic to support it. **Flag for
Julia.** ⓘ **What can be said:** a disallowed excess business loss *"is treated as a net operating
loss (NOL) carryover for subsequent years"*, so even where it bites it lands in the same bucket —
**but whether 2024's AGI moves was never tested, and "same bucket" is not the same as "same number".**

  trigger; the exposure is the same family.** ⛔ **Either way the form is not optional.**

   settles whether the 537,926 is an NOL or a restricted §1366(d)(2) carryover.
   ⚠️ **AND IT IS A 2024 QUESTION BEFORE IT IS A 2025 ONE.** If basis was short, part of that loss

   raises it.**
2. 🔴 **If basis was sufficient, it is now consumed** — and a 2025 distribution from a zero-basis
   corporation is **capital gain (§1368(b)(2), or §1368(c) where there is accumulated E&P)**.
   ⛔ **A flag for Julia, not a conclusion.**
3. 🟠 **Was a 2025 extension (Form 4868) filed?** If yes the deadline is **2026-10-15**; if no, the
   return has been late since 2026-04-15. **JK cannot have filed it** — he became a client
   2026-07-21.
4. 🟠 **Whether there is a Schedule C at all.** Double reads `1040-SCH C`; the 2024 return does not
   support it. Decides the home office and the vehicle treatment.
5. 🟠 **The 2025 vehicle sale** — whose vehicle, ever depreciated, and how many others.
6. 🟠 **Marketplace coverage or not** — pending with his broker.
7. **The NOL does not reduce self-employment tax**, and the failure-to-file penalty is computed on

   cheap.**
