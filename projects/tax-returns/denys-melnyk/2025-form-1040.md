**The premise she accepted:** we hold the corporation's 2024 return, so we know the loss and we know

### The chain, and why it closes

| Step | | Authority |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | The 2024 return deducted the **full** 630,737 as nonpassive, and **suspended nothing** — no carryover appears anywhere on the pages read | 📖 the filed return |
| 2 | **§1366(d)(1)** caps a loss deduction at *"the basis of your stock and loans from you to the corporation"*. **So the return ASSERTS basis of at least 630,737** | 📎 `i7203.pdf`, *Basis Limitations* |
| 3 | **§1367(a)** adjusts basis **"in the order listed"** — *increases under §1367(a)(1), decreases under §1367(a)(2)*: (1) increased by all income on the K-1 · (2) decreased by **distributions** · (3) decreased by **nondeductible expenses** · (4) decreased by **losses and deductions, last** | 📎 `i7203.pdf` |
| 4 | 🔑 **Each of steps (2)(3)(4) is "decreased (but not below zero)"** — verbatim, three times. So a loss that consumes basis leaves **zero**, not a negative | 📎 `i7203.pdf` |
| 5 | **Debt basis reads zero** — no loans from shareholders on Schedule L, K-1 item I blank, and no other line on the balance sheet carrying one (§2F) | 📖 both returns |

📎 *Read off the current PDFs on 2026-09-22: `irs.gov/pub/irs-pdf/i7203.pdf` (Instructions for Form
7203) and `irs.gov/pub/irs-pdf/i1120ssk.pdf` (Shareholder's Instructions for Schedule K-1). Not from

✅ **The obvious challenge, checked and closed: the §1.1367-1(g) election does NOT open a hole.** It
only lets a shareholder *"decrease your basis under (4) prior to decreasing your basis under (3)"* —
losses before nondeductible expenses, with the excess nondeductible amount carried forward. **It
reorders two reductions; it cannot leave residual stock basis.** *(`i7203.pdf`.)*

### ⚠️ The one thing this does NOT establish, stated so nobody over-claims it

**Accepting the return proves basis was *at least* the loss. It does not prove it was not MORE.**
If opening 2024 basis had exceeded 630,737, something would remain.

🔑 **Zero is still the figure to use, for three reasons that require auditing nobody — each bounded
to the search that produced it:**

1. **Nothing in the two returns we have read evidences more.** Form 7203 does not appear in the text
   extracted from either return, and no basis schedule appears on any page read. ⚠️ **That is a
   statement about a 44-page and a 36-page extraction with five partial pages (§6), not about the
   world.**
2. **Substantiating basis is the taxpayer's burden** — ⛔ **and he has not been asked.** The three
   questions only he can answer *(what he paid for the stock, contributions the corporation never
   recorded, a note or a payment under a guarantee)* are on record in the client file and have never
   been put to him.
3. ⛔ **Assuming a leftover would be INVENTING a figure** — which is precisely what her ruling
   forbids. **Zero is the only number a document supports.**

🟢 **The direction of the error, stated precisely.** A basis that is too low **understates what he
may deduct in a future year and can never overstate it** — so on the deduction side it cannot produce
an understated return. ⛔ **That is NOT the same as the "errs toward overpaying" argument §4A rejects
for the box-1 figure, and the two must not be read together**: there, an estimate is written onto an
income line and the return is wrong; here, a carry figure is set at the only documented value.

⚠️ **Two places a zero opening basis BITES, and neither is closed by this ruling:**

- 🔴 **A LOSS YEAR suspends the loss.** *"2025 has income"* is **the firm's chosen reporting figure
  (decision 3), not an established fact** — there is no 2025 K-1, the corporation lost 1,261,474 in
  2024, and §4A keeps open a fifth character in which the money is a **loan draw and not income at
  all**. ⛔ **If the K-1 eventually shows a loss, the loss is suspended AND the distribution becomes a
  §1368(b)(2) gain — both at once.**
- 🟠 **IF he ever had DEBT basis, the 2025 restoration goes to the DEBT first.** Under
  Reg. §1.1367-2(c) *(cited at `i1120ssk.pdf`)* a later year's net increase **restores reduced loan
  basis before stock basis**. **So on that branch 2025's income would rebuild debt basis and leave
  stock basis at zero — and the distribution would be fully taxable.** ⚖️ **Step 5 above is what
  keeps this shut, and step 5 is a reading of a balance sheet at two dates, not a measurement.**

### 🛑 THE ONE THING THAT MAKES THIS CONFUSING: box 1 and box 16D ARE NOT ALTERNATIVES

**They answer two different questions about two different things, and a real K-1 carries both:**

| | What it records | It is… |
|---|---|---|
| **Box 1** — ordinary business income | What the corporation **EARNED** and allocated to him | **INCOME.** He is taxed on it whether or not he took a cent out |
| **Box 16D** — distributions | What the corporation **PAID OUT** to him in cash | ⛔ **NOT INCOME.** It is him taking money that is already his — ⚠️ *true here because the corporation has **no accumulated E&P**; with AE&P a distribution above AAA would be a **dividend** under §1368(c). ⛔ **So "report it all as a distribution" is not ONE choice — it is TWO**: what goes in box 1, **and**
what goes in box 16D. 🔑 **And if you put the money in 16D while leaving box 1 at zero, the return
says the corporation earned him nothing and handed him 200,053 anyway — which is a CAPITAL GAIN.**
⚠️ **That is the trap this section exists for, and it is the literal reading of "todo como
distribución."**
