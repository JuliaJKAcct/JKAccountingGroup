## 5. Key facts & quirks

> ⚠️ **Order these by consequence — only the first FOUR are published.** Put first whatever would
> cause the worst mistake if someone didn't know it.

- 🔴 **The foreign block is the thing to get right, and the organizer answers it wrongly.** Oleksiy owns
  **100% of IT POINT LTD**, a Ukrainian wholesaler of machinery and electronic equipment (Chornomorsk,
  Odesa oblast; state register code `40029931`; functional currency UAH; two employees). The **2024
  return carried a full Form 5471** as a category **2, 4 and 5a** filer, with Schedules B, C, F, G, G-1,
  H, I-1, J, M, O and R. Yet the **2025 organizer answers "no" to foreign income and "no" to holding
  foreign accounts over the reporting threshold** — while the client uploaded IT POINT's 2025 statutory
  accounts, and a dividend resolution naming both a company and a personal Ukrainian bank account, to
  the same Drive folder. ⛔ **Never take the organizer's "no" here at face value.** The reporting test is
  the **highest balance at any point in the year**, and **signature authority counts even where the money
  is the company's**. Ask, get a number, and record the answer whichever way it goes — this is a
  signature-level exposure, not a computation. Also open: **Form 8938 does not appear in the 2024
  package**, and **Subpart F** deserves a specific look, because IT POINT wholesales the same category of
  goods AXDIGITAL sells and **Schedule M (related-party transactions) was filed in 2024**.
- 🔴 **They send documents by email and Google Drive, not through the portal — and the portal's own
  business section is empty.** The 2025 organizer is marked **completed**, but its entire business-income
  block, the vehicle-mileage questions and the home-office slide were **left blank**; the real data came
  as spreadsheets in a shared Drive folder. **Read the Drive folder and Gmail before concluding anything
  is missing**, and do not treat a blank organizer slide as an answer. Two of its answered slides also
  contradict documents in that folder (the foreign questions above; a 1095-A attached and simultaneously
  marked *not applicable*).
- 🔴 **Nine listed-property vehicles across three Schedule Cs, and the mileage never arrives in the form
  the return needs.** Each Turo Schedule C carries a fleet; Liudmyla's contracting Schedule C carries one
  car. What the client supplies are **odometer readings**, not the annual **business / commuting / other**
  split Form 4562 Part V requires. **Ask for the split explicitly, per vehicle**, or depreciation cannot
  be computed. A vehicle's business-use percentage also moves year to year and the client has claimed a
  jump to 100% on a car that was well under that in 2024.
- 🟡 **Marketplace coverage, two policies, and a large advance credit.** In 2025 the household ran a
  family policy for most of the year and a **second, child-only policy for the last two months**, so
  **two Forms 1095-A** exist and the same second-lowest-cost-silver-plan figure appears on **both** for
  the overlapping months. It cannot be counted twice — **Part IV allocation is required** (Part IV was
  already used on the 2024 return). Their income is far above 400% of the federal poverty line, where
  **no repayment cap applies**, so expect the advance credit to be repaid in substance. There is also an
  apparent **one-month coverage gap** for the child between the two policies.
- 🟡 **The home-office worksheet arrives incomplete every time, and always the same three lines.**
  Mortgage interest, homeowner's insurance and property taxes come back as **zero**, although the client
  uploads the Form 1098 that carries two of them and the escrow pays the third. The claimed office also
  **grew as a share of the apartment** between 2024 and 2025 with no stated reason, and most of the
  expense lines the client does fill in are **identical every month**, which reads as estimate rather than
  actual. Check all of it against the 1098 before keying.
- 🟡 **The Turo spreadsheets report income NET of Turo's fees.** Turo's own tax summary states that its
  **gross earnings** figure is what appears on the 1099-K. **Key the gross and deduct the fees**, or
  Schedule C line 1 will be short against the form the IRS receives. One spouse's figure is exactly net
  of fees; the other's does not reconcile either way and needs asking.
- 🟡 **Vehicles get destroyed and sold, and the paperwork lands on the wrong spouse.** A Turo car was a
  **total loss in 2025** — an **involuntary conversion** reportable on Form 4797, with a **§1033 deferral
  election available** if replaced in time. The car was on **one spouse's** Schedule C in 2024 but its
  settlement arrived in **the other spouse's** expense file. The 2024 return also carried Form 4797 and an
  **instalment sale on Form 6252**, so dispositions are a standing feature of this client, not a one-off.
- 🟡 **The P&L template the firm sends them is contaminated.** The workbook returned in Aug 2026 has a
  correctly completed profit-and-loss tab, but its **Balance sheet and Asset List tabs still hold a
  previous client's fixed-asset register and a balance sheet dated three years earlier.** Use the P&L tab
  only — and **the template itself needs fixing at source before it goes to anyone else.**
- 🟡 **A payment tagged to the wrong year is a live possibility.** The 2024 return ended in a balance due
  and included a **Form 9465 instalment-agreement request**. A payment of **exactly that balance** was then
  made in January 2026 and tagged as **2025 estimated tax**. Establish which year it was meant for before
  relying on it.
- ⓘ **Ping's transcripts of their calls are largely unusable.** The June 2026 recording is heavily garbled
  Russian/English auto-transcription; only fragments are legible. Do not build findings on it.
